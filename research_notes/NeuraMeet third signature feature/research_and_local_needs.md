# Research and Local Needs: Evidence for a Third NeuraMeet Signature Feature

Scope note: this covers academic HCI/CSCW/NLP/speech findings (2024 to 2026) and Pakistan-specific work and education realities. It excludes ideas already built or rejected (Promise Ledger, Live Ledger, anonymous AI devil's advocate) and the runners-up already considered (While You Were Gone recap, Viva/FYP panel mode, general Urdu English meeting intelligence). About 16 searches and fetches; several numbers come from search snippets or abstracts, not full-paper reads, and are marked where so.

## Q1. Which recent HCI/CSCW meeting prototypes show measurable benefits but have not become products?

### Takeaway
Three research lines have prototypes with measured effects and no mainstream product: (1) meeting-goal ("intentionality") reflection before and during meetings (Microsoft Research, CHI 2024 to CSCW 2025); (2) feedback on speaking balance and inclusion (Meeting Mediator, FairTalk, CHI 2026 Houtti et al.); (3) a human in the loop correcting AI meeting artefacts (speaker attribution correction, LLM critique-and-refine for summaries). The CHI 2026 inclusion study found that inside real organisations people used the tool for private reflection, not to exchange feedback. That matters for a hierarchical market like Pakistan.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft Research's "Intentional Meetings" project builds a goal-oriented meeting lifecycle that uses generative AI to "construct dynamic goal-driven interfaces" — [Microsoft Research, Intentional Meetings](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/intentional-meetings/)
- CHI 2024 paper "Mental Models of Meeting Goals: Supporting Intentionality in Meeting Technologies" grounds that work — [Microsoft Research publication](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/mental-models-of-meeting-goals-supporting-intentionality-in-meeting-technologies/); [camera-ready PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/chi24-774-authorcameraready.pdf)
- "What Does Success Look Like?" presents a Meeting Purpose Assistant (MPA). Before the meeting it asks organisers and attendees to state their goals, challenges and success criteria, then returns a short shareable summary. Brief reflective dialogue improved meeting preparation — [arXiv 2505.14370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14370); [MSR page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/what-does-success-look-like-catalyzing-meeting-intentionality-with-ai-assisted-prospective-reflection/)
- "Are We On Track? AI-Assisted Active and Passive Goal Reflection During Meetings": passive AI cues helped people stay focused without intruding. Active prompts triggered reflection right away but risked disrupting the conversation — [arXiv 2504.01082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01082); [MSR page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/are-we-on-track-ai-assisted-active-and-passive-goal-reflection-during-meetings/)
- CoExplorer technology probe (CSCW 2025): an adaptive generative-AI interface for intentional planning and running of video meetings. Related prototypes include Instant Recaps, Adaptive Meeting Handoff and Project Browsers — [MSR Intentional Meetings](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/intentional-meetings/); [MSR New Future of Work Report 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/New-Future-Of-Work-Report-2025.pdf)
- Houtti et al., "Opportunities and Barriers for AI Feedback on Meeting Inclusion in Socioorganizational Teams," CHI '26. Lab study (within-subjects, n=28) plus a field study at a consulting firm (n=10). The agent, built on the Induced Hypocrisy Procedure, "made speaking times more balanced and improved meeting quality." In the field, "organizational barriers led to its use for personal reflection rather than feedback exchange" — [arXiv 2601.11750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11750); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3791135)
- Meeting Mediator (MIT Media Lab): real-time sociometric feedback on speaking time. Overlapping speech was 31.8% of meeting time with the tool and 50.1% without — [MIT TR-616](https://hd.media.mit.edu/tech-reports/TR-616.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220879108_Meeting_mediator_Enhancing_group_collaborationusing_sociometric_feedback)
- "Immediate or Reflective?": real-time participation feedback made discussion "significantly less spontaneous," while its effects carried into later sessions as more expressiveness. This supports reflective (post-meeting) feedback over live nudges — [arXiv 2011.06529](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.06529v1)
- FairTalk (2025) predicts who wants to take a turn and shows it implicitly (a pseudo "leaning forward" effect). The lowest participant's speaking-turn ratio went up and time was spread more evenly than in the baseline — [arXiv 2505.20138](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20138)
- "Interactive In-Meeting Speaker Correction with Human Feedback": users fix speaker-attribution errors with short corrective feedback; the system updates the transcript and adds online speaker enrolments — [arXiv 2509.18377](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18377v2)

### Inferences
- The goal-reflection line (MPA, "Are We On Track?") is a research prototype with published measures. No meeting tool for South Asian users appears to offer it, so it is a credible "productise a CHI prototype" story. Its pre-meeting goal capture can reuse the existing `/api/prep` and the post-meeting summary pipeline at zero cost.
- Across Meeting Mediator, "Immediate or Reflective?" and Houtti CHI '26, real-time public feedback can hurt spontaneity, and organisations push people toward private use. A Pakistan-appropriate design is therefore private, post-meeting and owned by the user, not a live public scoreboard. This also matches the lesson from the rejected devil's advocate idea (psychological safety).

### Gaps
- I did not read the full MPA or "Are We On Track?" papers, so I have no sample sizes or effect sizes for them. The report should cite them only qualitatively unless someone reads the PDFs.
- I found no evidence that Teams, Zoom or Meet ship goal-reflection features based on this research (Microsoft may have shipped pieces in Copilot; not verified).

## Q2. Documented failure modes of AI meeting summaries and transcripts, and proposed fixes that nobody ships

### Takeaway
The main failures are well documented: omissions are the dominant error, misattribution exists, ASR hallucinations get through, automatic metrics hide errors, and users rarely verify. Code-switched Urdu English roughly doubles Whisper's error rate and breaks at language switch points. The proposed fixes are human-in-the-loop correction, critique-and-refine, and summaries with speaker attribution. None of them is shipped as a participant-facing verification step. Users do not check on their own (23 of 319), so verification has to be designed into the workflow, not left to the user.

### Cited Findings
- Meeting summaries from LLMs "remain error-prone, often producing outputs with hallucinations, omissions, and irrelevancies." FRAME reduces hallucination and omission by 2 of 5 points on MESA and keeps speaker attribution more consistent (Findings of EMNLP 2025) — [ACL Anthology 2025.findings-emnlp.1094](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1094.pdf)
- "What's Wrong? Refining Meeting Summaries with LLM Feedback" (COLING 2025) uses a two-phase multi-LLM process that mimics human review: identify mistakes, then refine. Its error taxonomy includes misattributed statements and omitted necessary mentions — [ACL Anthology 2025.coling-main.143](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.143/)
- ROUGE and BERTScore are only a weak proxy for meeting-summary quality; human evaluation is still the gold standard (COLING 2025 industry track) — [ACL Anthology 2025.coling-industry.48](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-industry.48.pdf)
- A secondary article reports that 97% of summaries from one tested (older) model missed information, on a sample of 35 meetings. It cites Kirstein et al. (Findings of EMNLP 2024) and TofuEval (Tang et al., NAACL 2024), and says automatic scores masked errors in about a third of comparisons. The article gives no full citation for the 97% figure, so treat it as unverified — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/your-ai-meeting-summary-is-probably-missing-something)
- Lee et al., CHI 2025 (Microsoft Research and CMU, 319 knowledge workers): the more confidence people had in the AI, the less critical thinking they applied. A common reason for not checking was that the task felt too trivial — [Microsoft Research PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf). The figure "only 23 of 319 checked a cited source" comes from the secondary HackerNoon article and was not checked against the paper — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/your-ai-meeting-summary-is-probably-missing-something)
- Koenecke et al., "Careless Whisper: Speech-to-Text Hallucination Harms," FAccT 2024: 1.4% of audio segments produced a hallucination sequence (2023 experiments), and 38% of those hallucinations included explicit harms (violence, false associations, false authority). Hallucinations happen more for speakers with longer non-vocal pauses — [arXiv 2402.08021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.08021); [Cornell Chronicle](https://www.cornell.edu/stories/2024/06/ai-speech-text-can-hallucinate-violent-language)
- Urdu English code-switching: Whisper-large-v3 often transliterates or translates English into Urdu script instead of transcribing literally. Reported WER is 0.532 on code-switched segments versus 0.289 without code-switching (from search-result snippets; the source is either arXiv 2508.09865 or SSRN 6959921). That work proposes a Switch Point Error Rate (SPER) metric to isolate errors at language boundaries — [arXiv 2508.09865](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09865); [SSRN, Haroon](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/6959921.pdf?abstractid=6959921&mirid=1)
- "WER We Stand: Benchmarking Urdu ASR Models" (COLING 2025) benchmarks Urdu ASR — [ACL Anthology 2025.coling-main.397](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.397.pdf). UrduSpeech (2026) is a 156-hour corpus that targets RTL-script constraints and frequent code-switching — [arXiv 2605.17846](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17846v1)
- Errors compound: the summariser inherits ASR errors and speaker-attribution mistakes from earlier stages — [ACL Anthology 2025.coling-main.143](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.143.pdf)
- CHI 2025 ownership research: people disown work when they only approve AI suggestions, and keep ownership when they lead, iterate or rewrite — [arXiv 2609.20658](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20658v1). A CHI 2026 study on multi-user and AI document editing found that AI comments became shared "action items" that participants accepted or rejected — [ACM DL 10.1145/3772318.3790648](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790648)

### Inferences
- The unshipped fix is participant-side verification of AI minutes: each attendee confirms or corrects the statements and decisions attributed to them. The NLP fixes (FRAME, critique-and-refine) are model-side and still leave the omission problem, while Lee CHI 2025 shows users will not verify unprompted. NeuraMeet already uses quote-backed evidence in Promise Ledger, so linking each minutes sentence to transcript evidence reuses existing plumbing.
- Groq's Whisper endpoint can return per-segment confidence fields (`avg_logprob`, `no_speech_prob`, `compression_ratio`) with `verbose_json`. That would allow flagging likely hallucinated or code-switch-garbled segments for free. This is my inference from the OpenAI-compatible API shape; verify it against the Groq docs before committing.
- Thesis metrics are straightforward: seeded-error detection rate (plant k known errors into minutes, measure how many participants catch them), WER/SPER before and after participant correction, and a measure of how often unsupported sentences reach the final minutes.

### Gaps
- No primary source for the "97% of summaries missing information" figure was retrieved. The 53.2% vs 28.9% WER split comes from snippets, not a full-paper read.
- I found no study on whether AI summaries specifically omit dissent or minority positions. That is plausible given the omission findings, but I found no direct evidence.

## Q3. Pakistan specifics: how meetings run and which needs are unique

### Takeaway
Four Pakistan-specific realities stand out. (1) Formal minutes culture: federal Secretariat Instructions require brief minutes of decisions only, record individual views "only on request," and require circulation and confirmation within fixed deadlines. (2) High power distance, which predicts employee silence. (3) WhatsApp as the de facto official follow-up channel, with some provincial bans. (4) Frequent connectivity loss from load shedding and backbone outages, on a mobile-first base (68% smartphone adoption, about 60% mobile broadband penetration).

### Cited Findings
- Federal Secretariat Instructions (issued under Rule 5(15) of the Rules of Business, 1973): meetings should be business-like, with "brief minutes recorded mentioning only the salient points considered and the decisions taken, and individual view points should not be recorded except on request" — [Establishment Division, Secretariat Instructions 2021](https://www.establishment.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/Secretariat%20Instructions%20Edition%202021.pdf); [older edition](https://establishment.gov.pk/userfiles1/file/Secretariat%20Instructions.pdf). Punjab has its own Manual of Secretariat Instructions 2023 — [Punjab PDF](https://schools.punjab.gov.pk/system/files/MANUAL%20OF%20SECRETARIAT%20INSTRUCTIONS%20(Updated%202023)_1_0.pdf)
- Board procedure (Privatisation Commission regulations): after the Chairman approves, the Secretary circulates minutes to members "for confirmation within three working days of approval" — [privatisation.gov.pk](https://privatisation.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/Regulations_Conduct_Board_Meeting2019_09042021).pdf). Council of Common Interests: minutes go to members within seven working days — [CCI Rules of Procedure](https://ipc.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/CCI/Rules%20of%20Procedure%20of%20the%20CCI,%202010%20-%20amended%2022_4_2021.pdf)
- Power distance and silence: a study of school teachers in Sargodha found that abusive leadership, ostracism and power distance positively predicted employee silence, and that power distance moderated those effects. The authors link low voice behaviour to high unemployment and economic pressure — [IJELM, Hipatia Press](https://hipatiapress.com/hpjournals/index.php/ijelm/article/view/10373). A Pakistan vs Australia comparison found the silence and ethical-leadership relationship was stronger in Pakistan — [University of Wollongong thesis](https://ro.uow.edu.au/articles/thesis/Influence_of_followers_voice_and_silence_on_leaders_ethical_behaviour/30749036)
- WhatsApp as an official channel: one account describes an employee in 52 work-related groups; Punjab officials use WhatsApp at scale; the KP government designated certain WhatsApp groups as official correspondence — [Aurora (Dawn)](https://aurora.dawn.com/news/1144706); [Aaj English](https://english.aaj.tv/news/10391650). The Punjab government banned WhatsApp for official communication in government offices in February 2020, which shows a policy tension over records and security — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2020/02/17/punjab-govt-bans-whatsapp-in-government-offices/); [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2157682/entertainment)
- Connectivity: an August 2025 PTCL backbone outage cut national internet to about 20% of normal and hit Jazz, Zong and Telenor — [Digital Rights Monitor](https://digitalrightsmonitor.pk/pakistan-internet-outage-2025/). When load shedding drains tower backup batteries, mobile towers lose signal, which disrupts online classes and remote work — [PhoneWorld](https://www.phoneworld.com.pk/load-shedding-disrupts-mobile-and-internet-services-across-pakistan/); [Aaj TV](https://english.aaj.tv/news/330456984/internet-power-outages-deepen-crisis-across-pakistan). Pakistan was reported to have the world's highest economic losses from internet shutdowns in 2024, about $1.62B (secondary source) — [TechBazaar](https://blog.techbazaar.pk/internet-outage-in-pakistan/)
- Mobile-first base (PTA Annual Report 2024-25, via press): over 200M subscribers, over 150M broadband connections, 68% smartphone adoption, mobile broadband reaching 81% of the population, and NGMS penetration of 60.32% by December 2025 — [CW Pakistan](https://cwpakistan.com/pta-annual-report-2024-25-shows-growth-in-telecom-subscribers-revenues-and-infrastructure/); [Profit / Pakistan Today](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/01/20/pakistans-mobile-subscriber-base-tops-200-million-by-end-of-2025-pta-data-shows/)

### Inferences
- Minutes in Pakistani government and university bodies carry legal weight, so there is a formal "confirm the minutes" step. An AI tool that drafts minutes in the decisions-only format and then runs a deadline-based confirmation by members is a direct digital version of a real rule, which is rare for an FYP. The "individual views only on request" rule maps to a "Record my view" button that lets a member formally enter their dissent. That suits a high power distance culture better than anonymous AI dissent: it is user-initiated, owned and procedural.
- Load shedding means people drop out mid-sentence, not just miss content. While You Were Gone covers catching up. The uncovered gap is losing your own contribution while offline. Store-and-forward local audio capture (record locally, upload when the connection returns, merge into the transcript by timestamp) is distinct and technically showy.
- WhatsApp is where follow-up actually happens, and the Punjab ban shows that unofficial channels cause record-keeping anxiety. A "WhatsApp-ready" handoff of confirmed minutes (wa.me share links, no paid API) keeps the official record in NeuraMeet. It is a useful companion, but probably too thin to be a signature feature on its own.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve HEC documents on how university statutory bodies (Syndicate, Academic Council, BASR) confirm minutes. The pattern is likely similar to government rules, but that is unverified.
- I found no survey quantifying how Pakistani SMEs record meeting outcomes, or what share use WhatsApp for follow-up. Evidence is anecdotal or press-level.
- I found no data on how often load shedding interrupts online meetings or classes specifically, only general impact reporting.

## Q4. Candidate feature directions and which allow clean thesis evaluation

### Takeaway
Six directions are grounded in both research and Pakistani context. The strongest for an FYP panel are (A) Verified Minutes and (E) Offline-Safe Voice. Each has a clear research gap, a local rule or pain behind it, a demo moment and a hard metric.

### Cited Findings (evidence mapping; sources as cited above)
**A. Verified Minutes ("Tasdeeq"): participant-confirmed, evidence-linked official minutes**
- Problem: AI minutes omit, misattribute and hallucinate, and users do not verify. Pakistani bodies legally require minutes to be circulated and confirmed.
- Design: generate minutes in Secretariat-Instructions format (salient points and decisions). Each sentence links to its supporting transcript span, and sentences without support are flagged. Each attendee gets a review link for the lines attributed to them and can confirm, correct or "record my view" (dissent entered on request, per the rule). A confirmation deadline (for example 3 or 7 days) and a confirmation status per member are tracked.
- Evidence: [FRAME, EMNLP Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1094.pdf); [What's Wrong?, COLING 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.143/); [Lee et al. CHI 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf); [Careless Whisper FAccT 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.08021); [Secretariat Instructions](https://www.establishment.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/Secretariat%20Instructions%20Edition%202021.pdf); [Board confirmation in 3 working days](https://privatisation.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/Regulations_Conduct_Board_Meeting2019_09042021).pdf); [Ownership CHI research](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20658v1)
- Metrics: seeded-error catch rate; share of minutes sentences evidence-supported before and after; time to full confirmation; SUS and trust scores against unverified AI minutes.
- Overlap risk: Promise Ledger already has owner confirm or reject of matches. The difference is that this covers the whole minutes, every attendee, and formal confirmation.

**B. Code-Switch Confidence Lens: an uncertainty-aware Urdu English transcript**
- Problem: WER roughly doubles on code-switched speech (0.532 vs 0.289), English gets transliterated into Urdu script, and hallucinations reach summaries.
- Design: use Whisper segment confidence fields plus script-switch detection to highlight risky spans. The speaker corrects them with one tap, and the summary and RAG use only verified spans.
- Evidence: [arXiv 2508.09865](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09865); [SSRN SPER metric](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/6959921.pdf?abstractid=6959921&mirid=1); [In-meeting speaker correction](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18377v2); [Careless Whisper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.08021)
- Metrics: WER and SPER before and after correction on a labelled bilingual test set; precision and recall of the risk flags against actual errors.
- Overlap risk: close to the "Urdu English meeting intelligence" runner-up. It is best positioned as a sub-component of A, not a standalone feature.

**C. Meeting Purpose and Goal Attainment (productising MSR intentionality research)**
- Problem: unclear meeting goals. MSR prototypes improved preparation and focus, and passive cues beat active interruptions.
- Design: a pre-meeting purpose prompt for organiser and attendees (via `/api/prep`), a passive in-call goal indicator, and a post-meeting "goal met / partly / not" score backed by transcript evidence, tracked across meetings.
- Evidence: [CHI 2024 Mental Models](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/mental-models-of-meeting-goals-supporting-intentionality-in-meeting-technologies/); [MPA arXiv 2505.14370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14370); [Are We On Track? arXiv 2504.01082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01082)
- Metrics: pre/post goal-clarity ratings, share of meetings with stated goals, goal-attainment agreement between AI and attendees.

**D. Private Voice Mirror plus "Record My View" (hierarchy-aware inclusion)**
- Problem: high power distance in Pakistan predicts silence. Public real-time feedback reduces spontaneity, and organisations push such tools toward private use.
- Design: a private post-meeting reflection for each user (speaking share, interruptions, turns that were started but cut off) visible only to that user, plus a formal "record my view" option for adding a dissent or concern to the minutes under their own name.
- Evidence: [Houtti et al. CHI '26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11750); [Meeting Mediator](https://hd.media.mit.edu/tech-reports/TR-616.pdf); [Immediate or Reflective?](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.06529v1); [FairTalk](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20138); [Sargodha power distance study](https://hipatiapress.com/hpjournals/index.php/ijelm/article/view/10373)
- Metrics: speaking-time Gini across repeated meetings, number of recorded views by junior members, self-reported psychological safety.
- Overlap risk: the existing per-speaker engagement insights (C3). The new part is that it is private and that dissent can be formally recorded.

**E. Offline-Safe Voice: store-and-forward audio during connection drops**
- Problem: load shedding and backbone outages drop participants mid-contribution, and what they said is lost from the transcript.
- Design: the browser records the user's own mic locally in chunks while in a call. If the connection drops, recording continues; when the connection returns, the chunks are uploaded to Supabase Storage, transcribed with Groq Whisper, and merged into the transcript by timestamp, marked as recovered audio.
- Evidence: [PTCL outage Aug 2025](https://digitalrightsmonitor.pk/pakistan-internet-outage-2025/); [load shedding and towers](https://www.phoneworld.com.pk/load-shedding-disrupts-mobile-and-internet-services-across-pakistan/); [PTA mobile-first data](https://cwpakistan.com/pta-annual-report-2024-25-shows-growth-in-telecom-subscribers-revenues-and-infrastructure/)
- Metrics: share of speech recovered in simulated-outage trials (network throttled in DevTools), merge timestamp error, transcript completeness against ground truth.
- Risk: Supabase free Storage quota and Groq rate limits; keep chunks small and per-user.

**F. WhatsApp-Ready Handoff (companion, not signature)**
- Problem: follow-up actually happens on WhatsApp, and official-record anxiety exists (Punjab 2020 ban, KP official groups).
- Design: one-tap wa.me share of confirmed minutes or action items in Urdu or English, linking back to the official record in NeuraMeet.
- Evidence: [Aurora/Dawn](https://aurora.dawn.com/news/1144706); [ProPakistani ban](https://propakistani.pk/2020/02/17/punjab-govt-bans-whatsapp-in-government-offices/)
- Metrics: follow-up completion rate, click-through back to the record.

### Inferences
- Ranking by novelty, defensibility, demo value and zero-cost feasibility: A (best fit; it combines B and the "record my view" part of D into one story grounded in a real Pakistani rule plus CHI/EMNLP/FAccT evidence), then E (technically impressive, clearly local, measurable), then C (cleanest "productised CHI prototype" story, less local), then D (sensitive, overlaps C3), then B (overlaps a runner-up), then F (supporting feature).
- A and E both allow a controlled evaluation with an objective metric (seeded-error catch rate; speech recovery rate) plus a small user study, which panels tend to reward.
- All six fit the zero-cost stack: Gemini/Groq for generation, Groq Whisper for ASR, pgvector or plain Postgres for evidence links, Supabase Storage for audio, Stream only for the call itself.

### Gaps
- I did not search CHI/CSCW 2024 to 2026 work on async alternatives to meetings or on video-meeting fatigue, so ideas from those areas are not covered here.
- I did not check whether Otter, Fireflies, Read.ai or tl;dv already ship participant confirmation of minutes. Novelty claims for A should stay narrow ("none of the tools we reviewed") until someone checks.
- Groq Whisper `verbose_json` confidence fields (B) and browser MediaRecorder behaviour during network loss alongside Stream's own media handling (E) need technical verification during `/architect`.
