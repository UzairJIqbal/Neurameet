# Build a Promise Ledger That Remembers Meetings

NeuraMeet should add a **Promise Ledger**: a cross-meeting memory that pulls every *decision* and every *commitment* ("Ali: main Friday tak survey results bhej doon ga") out of each Urdu-English meeting, carries them forward, and on every later meeting automatically marks each one **kept, still open, overdue, or reversed**, always with the exact quote and timestamp as evidence. The pitch for the panel is one sentence: *"NeuraMeet remembers what your team decided and promised, in English or Urdu, and shows you with the exact quote when a promise is kept, broken, or a decision is quietly reversed."* This is the strongest choice because it attacks the most frequently documented meeting pain (people leave meetings unsure of next steps, and meetings mostly produce more meetings), because the research that proposes it (decision tracking, commitment detection) has not been productized by Zoom, Meet, Teams, Webex, Otter, Fireflies or Read.ai in any source this research found, and because the code-mixed Urdu-English angle has no commercial competitor at all, only a Pakistani research project. It also runs entirely after the meeting on transcripts NeuraMeet already produces, so it adds about two or three free-tier LLM calls per meeting and reuses Gemini/Groq, pgvector, action items and follow-ups without any new provider. The honest caveat: decision-reversal detection on its own is *not* unique, because small startups (Sentra, Proactor AI) already do versions of it, so the defensible claim is the combination: commitment fulfilment verified from later transcripts, plus reversal alerts, plus code-mixed Urdu-English input, plus a quote behind every verdict.

## Almost everything NeuraMeet already has is now standard

The starting point is uncomfortable: none of NeuraMeet's current AI features would stand out to a panel that has used commercial tools. Zoom's AI Companion 3.0 already ships meeting prep from past meetings, cross-platform "agentic retrieval" search, agenda tracking and even a "Free up my time" feature that suggests meetings to skip ([Zoom News](https://news.zoom.com/ai-companion-3-0-and-zoom-workplace/)). Teams' **Facilitator agent** runs agendas with a timer, captures decisions and creates follow-ups for Copilot-licensed users ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/teams/copilot/facilitator-in-microsoft-teams-meetings)). Read.ai already sells per-participant engagement, sentiment, talk-time balance and bias indicators, which overlaps directly with NeuraMeet's per-speaker engagement insights ([Read.ai Support](https://support.read.ai/hc/en-us/articles/4406653674003-About-Sentiment-Engagement-and-the-Read-Score)). Otter's "Hey Otter" Meeting Agent answers questions over past meetings by voice and creates action items ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/otterais-meeting-agent-can-schedule-calls-and-write-emails-for-you-130048189.html)), and Otter's "My Action Items" already tracks action items across meetings ([SalesTechStar](https://salestechstar.com/?p=238379)). Even Urdu is covered: **Fireflies lists Urdu ("ur") and a Multi-Language Mode for meetings where speakers switch languages** ([Fireflies Guide](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/2973706448-learn-about-fireflies-supported-languages)).

So summaries, action items, RAG chat, cross-meeting search, prep, engagement analytics, catch-up, agenda timers, voice agents, translation and Urdu transcription are all off the table as "the unique feature." The research notes converge on one conclusion: the standout has to be about **reasoning across meetings**, not analysing one meeting better. The gap is not *listing* action items, which everyone does, but **closing the loop**: checking, from what was later said, whether a promise was actually kept and whether a decision still stands.

## Meeting follow-through is the pain incumbents leave open

The demand evidence is strong at the level of the problem, though weaker at the level of exact numbers. In Atlassian's survey of 5,000 knowledge workers, **meetings were ineffective 72% of the time and 77% said meetings mainly create more meetings**, with Fortune summarising the outcome as "no decisions were made, and nobody knows what's expected of them" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/03/21/meetings-productivity-ineffective-atlassian-report)). Secondary reporting of the same research puts **54% of workers leaving meetings without knowing next steps or who owns tasks** ([Reworked](https://www.reworked.co/collaboration-productivity/atlassian-found-meetings-are-unproductive-heres-what-needs-to-change/)). Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index found that **57% of meetings are ad hoc calls with no calendar invite** ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/06/17/microsoft-remote-work-meetings)). That matters for design: a memory feature has to infer context from the meeting content, because calendar-based prep misses most meetings. The "77% meetings beget meetings" figure is the most direct argument for the reversal half of the feature. Teams re-discuss topics they already settled, and nothing tells them so.

Two caveats keep this honest. First, the widely repeated statistics that "44%" or "40%" of action items never get completed come only from vendor blogs with no traceable study, so they must not appear in the FYP report ([acta.ai](https://acta.ai/blogs/meeting-follow-through-why-action-items-dont-get-done)). Neither should the "63% of verbal commitments are never documented" claim ([skills.sh](https://skills.sh/sixtysecondsapp/use60/meeting-digest-truth-extractor)). Second, the Atlassian numbers reach us through press and aggregators, not the original report. The case for the feature rests on the *pattern*, which is consistent across Atlassian, Microsoft and the HBR senior-manager survey, not on any single precise figure.

The Pakistani angle sharpens the need rather than merely localising it. Urdu-English code-switching ("Urlish") is normal in Pakistani institutional speech ([IJLC](https://ijlc.wum.edu.pk/index.php/ojs/article/view/268)). Lightweight Whisper models still post **33.68% WER on Urdu even at the Small size** ([arXiv 2508.09865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09865)), and general code-switching research reports that monolingual ASR shows 30 to 50% higher error on code-switched audio ([Gladia](https://gladia.io/blog/what-is-code-switching-in-speech-recognition)). When the transcript is that noisy, a bare summary line such as "Ali will send the survey" is hard to trust. A ledger entry that shows the original code-mixed quote and the timestamp lets the user verify it in seconds. Trust in AI meeting output is already a top complaint: accuracy is the most-cited Otter dislike on G2, and it gets worse for non-native accents ([G2 Otter reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/otter-ai/reviews_and_filters)). Evidence-backed verdicts address that directly.

## Uniqueness holds for the combination, not for each part

The table below separates what was verified in this research from what was not. "Not found" means no shipping product turned up in this pass. It is not proof that none exists, and the FYP write-up should phrase it that way.

| Component | Who already has it | Status for NeuraMeet's claim |
|---|---|---|
| Action items tracked across meetings | Otter "My Action Items", Fellow, Circleback ([SalesTechStar](https://salestechstar.com/?p=238379); [Circleback](https://circleback.ai/how-to/track-meeting-action-items)) | **Verified: not unique** |
| Decisions extracted and logged | Teams Facilitator captures decisions ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/teams/copilot/facilitator-in-microsoft-teams-meetings)) | **Verified: not unique** |
| Flagging when a new decision reverses an old one | Sentra, a startup ([Sentra](https://www.sentra.app/articles/ai-meeting-memory)); Proactor AI triggers on contradictions to past meetings ([Proactor](https://proactor.ai/blog/?p=3369)) | **Verified: exists at startups, absent from the majors and mainstream notetakers checked** |
| Automatically verifying that a commitment was *fulfilled*, using evidence from later meeting transcripts | Not found in any tool researched. The closest academic task is SemEval-2025 PromiseEval, which checks promises against evidence in the ESG domain ([ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/transition-to-people-yaml/2025.semeval-1.225.pdf)) | **Unverified absence: strongest novelty claim** |
| Decision/commitment memory over Urdu-English code-mixed meetings, with Roman Urdu output | No commercial product found. Only CLE Pakistan's research project on Urdu meeting minutes ([CLE](https://cle.org.pk/research/projects)) | **Unverified absence: strong local differentiator** |
| Gong, Fathom, tl;dv, Krisp, Granola, Avoma, Fellow commitment features | Not individually checked | **Gap: check Gong's "next steps" tracking before claiming "no product does this"** |

The research-literature position supports the framing. *Meeting Decision Tracker* (2022) proposed detecting decision utterances and rewriting them so they can be read without the surrounding meeting, explicitly for "extensive tracking of past discussions" ([arXiv 2210.11374](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.11374)). TU Graz has explored LLMs that track how decisions develop across transcripts ([TU Graz](https://tugraz.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/towards-group-decision-support-with-llm-based-meeting-analysis/)). Commitment detection is known to be hard: models perform poorly on CommitmentBank-style data ([ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/transition-to-people-yaml/2025.semeval-1.225.pdf)). The notes found **no controlled study of cross-meeting contradiction or reversal detection**. That makes a small evaluation by NeuraMeet a genuine contribution rather than a replication. The safe public claim is therefore: *"No major meeting platform (Zoom, Meet, Teams, Webex) or mainstream notetaker (Otter, Fireflies, Read.ai) offers automatic, evidence-backed commitment-fulfilment tracking across meetings, and none offers decision/commitment memory designed for Urdu-English code-mixed speech, as of October 2026 in our review."*

## The ledger fits inside the free tiers with room to spare

Feasibility is the easiest part of the case, because the ledger is a **post-meeting** pipeline. It never listens live, so it avoids every tight real-time limit in the stack. The binding constraints, and how the ledger sits against them, are summarised below.

| Resource | Free-tier limit | Ledger usage per meeting |
|---|---|---|
| Groq Whisper | 20 RPM, 7,200 audio-sec/hour, 28,800/day, 25 MB file ([Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits); [Groq STT](https://console.groq.com/docs/speech-to-text)) | **Zero extra.** Reuses the F-step transcript. |
| Gemini Flash / Flash-Lite | Free of charge ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Per-model RPM/RPD is no longer published, and third-party reports range from roughly 20 to 1,500 RPD ([yingtu.ai](https://yingtu.ai/en/blog/google-gemini-api-free-tier-limits-2026)) | **About 2 to 3 calls**: one extraction (can be merged into the existing `/api/ai/process` prompt) and one batched judge call over the top-k candidates |
| Gemini embeddings | Free ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)) | One batched embed of perhaps 5 to 20 short ledger items |
| Groq LLM fallback | 8K TPM, 200K TPD on gpt-oss ([Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)) | A compact judge prompt (top-5 candidates, quotes only) stays well under 8K tokens |
| Supabase pgvector | 500 MB DB ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)) | Ledger rows with one embedding each come to a few KB per item, which is negligible |
| Vercel Hobby | 300 s function limit, 4.5 MB body ([Vercel limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations)) | Text-only calls finish in seconds |

Two real risks remain. First, the **Gemini daily quota is unknown** until it is read from AI Studio on the project's own key. One report claims Gemini 2.5 Flash fell to about 20 requests per day in December 2025 ([yingtu.ai](https://yingtu.ai/en/blog/google-gemini-api-free-tier-limits-2026)). The existing Groq fallback covers this, and Flash-Lite should be the default for the judge call. Second, **Supabase Free pauses a project after one week of inactivity** ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)), so a keep-alive ping is needed before the viva. Privacy is worth one slide: Gemini's free tier uses submitted content "to improve our products" ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). NeuraMeet should disclose that, and it can contrast its native, consent-visible recording with bolt-on bots, which are now the subject of the consolidated Otter class action ([NPR/NHPR](https://www.nhpr.org/2025-08-15/class-action-suit-claims-otter-ai-secretly-records-private-work-conversations)).

### Build outline that extends rather than duplicates

The build reuses the pipeline that already exists, transcript → `/api/ai/process` → embeddings → follow-ups, and adds one stage after it. In line with AGENTS.md, it should start with `/architect`, because it needs **new tables and at least one new API route**, and the API contract list must be amended explicitly rather than improvised.

| Step | What it does | Reuses |
|---|---|---|
| L0 `/architect` | Lock the schema, the judge prompt, the relation labels, and the new route name (for example `GET /api/ledger`), and add it to the AGENTS.md contract | Architecture rules |
| L1 Schema | `ledger_items` (meeting_id, owner user scope, kind = decision/commitment, decontextualized text, speaker, assignee, due date, original quote, transcript timestamp, embedding, status) and `ledger_links` (from_item, to_item, relation = supports/fulfils/contradicts/supersedes, evidence quote, confidence, user_confirmed) | Supabase + pgvector, `meeting_chunks` conventions |
| L2 Extraction | Extend the `/api/ai/process` prompt to return decisions and commitments as decontextualized sentences with the original code-mixed quote and timestamp. Link commitments to existing `action_items` instead of duplicating them | Gemini/Groq via AI SDK, try/catch + fallback |
| L3 Matching | Embed the new items, then for each one retrieve the top-5 nearest *open* items from the user's earlier meetings in pgvector. Make one batched judge call that returns a relation, a confidence and a supporting quote for each pair, and drop anything below a confidence threshold | `/api/search` user scoping, Gemini embeddings |
| L4 Status engine | Map the judge output onto item status: fulfilled (evidence quote), superseded (newer decision), overdue (due date passed with no evidence), open. Push overdue commitments into `follow_ups` | Follow-ups pipeline |
| L5 UI | A "Ledger" tab in Meeting Intelligence, reversal/fulfilment cards that show both quotes side by side with "Confirm / Not a match" buttons (human in the loop, and it also produces evaluation labels), and a status chip on the Follow-ups page. Optional Roman Urdu / English toggle on item text | Existing tabs, `useLoadingTimeout` + `LoadError`, design tokens |
| L6 Prep hook | Feed open and overdue items into the existing prep suggestions so the next meeting opens with "3 open promises from last time" | `/api/prep` |

Steps L1 to L5 form the minimum demoable core. L6 and the Roman Urdu toggle are polish.

## A four-minute demo and a small study make it defensible

The demo should use **seeded, uploaded transcripts for the earlier meetings and a live or uploaded third meeting**, so that it never depends on a recording working on the day. Write the transcripts in natural Urdu-English, because the code-mixing is itself part of the argument.

| Time | Action | What the panel sees |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:40 | Problem framing: "Atlassian: 72% of meetings ineffective, 77% create more meetings. Every tool lists action items; none tells you if they happened." | One slide, then NeuraMeet's dashboard |
| 0:40–1:30 | Open seeded Meeting 1 (FYP group): "Hum Vercel free tier pe deploy karenge, no paid hosting" and "Ali: main Friday tak survey results share kar doon ga." Open its Ledger tab. | Two ledger items, each with the original quote, timestamp and a decontextualized English line |
| 1:30–2:30 | Upload Meeting 2: "Let's just move to AWS paid, Vercel limits are annoying." The survey is never mentioned. | A red **"Reverses decision from Meeting 1"** card with both quotes side by side and Confirm / Not a match buttons. Ali's commitment flips to **Overdue: no evidence in 1 later meeting** and appears in Follow-ups |
| 2:30–3:20 | Upload or live-record Meeting 3: "Ali: survey bhej diya tha, results group mein hain." | Ali's commitment turns **Fulfilled**, with the evidence quote and timestamp linked |
| 3:20–4:00 | Open prep for Meeting 4, then show the evaluation slide | "1 reversed decision to confirm, 0 open promises", plus precision/recall and study results |

For academic credibility the evaluation should have two layers, since most of the related research ideas lack outcome data. **Layer one is a technical accuracy test.** Build a hand-labelled set of roughly 30 to 50 cross-meeting pairs: kept commitments, broken commitments, reversed decisions, and near-miss distractors (same topic, no contradiction). At least half should be code-mixed Urdu-English. Have two people label each pair independently, report Cohen's kappa for agreement, and then report the judge's precision and recall per relation type, separately for English and code-mixed input. That split directly tests the Urdu claim. **Layer two is a small within-subjects user study** with 6 to 10 participants (classmates or a real FYP group). Each participant answers the same task, "Which promises from the last three meetings are still open, and did we reverse any decision?", once with NeuraMeet's existing summaries and search and once with the Ledger, in counterbalanced order. Measure answer accuracy, time to complete, NASA-TLX workload, SUS and a trust-in-output rating. These sample sizes are in line with published probe studies in this space: the CHI 2025 goal-reflection probe used 15 knowledge workers ([arXiv 2504.01082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01082)) and MeetingCoach followed 8 teams over 4 weeks ([MSR](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2021/01/MeetingCoach_CHI2021_cameraready.pdf)). The study should claim *task-level* gains only, not better meetings. A preregistered field experiment with 361 employees and 7,196 meetings found that goal nudges did **not** significantly improve meeting effectiveness ([arXiv 2602.16939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16939)). A panel member who knows that literature will respect the restraint.

### Risks the panel will probe

The biggest technical risk is **false positives**. Telling someone they broke a promise when they did not damages trust faster than a missed detection, so the UI should say "no evidence found yet" rather than "broken", default to human confirmation, and keep the confidence threshold conservative. The second risk is that **ASR errors propagate**. On code-switched audio, Whisper large-v3 is reported to translate or transliterate English words into Urdu script, and an unverified figure puts its WER at about 0.53 with code-switching against 0.29 without ([IBA repository](https://ir.iba.edu.pk/fyp-bscs/50); [arXiv 2605.17846](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17846)). The tables should therefore present the code-mixed results separately and avoid overstating them. The third risk is that the **novelty claim gets narrowed by a competitor nobody checked**. Gong, Fathom, tl;dv and Krisp were not individually verified, and the reversal-detection component already exists at Sentra and Proactor, so the write-up should cite those startups openly as related work rather than hope the panel has not heard of them. Finally, the ledger stores team members' promises with names attached, so it should stay strictly scoped to the meeting's owner and participants, following the existing user-scoped search.

## Two runners-up worth keeping in reserve

**Runner-up 1: "Urlish-native meeting intelligence."** This would make summaries, action items and RAG answers preserve the code-mixing, with a per-user choice of English, Urdu script or Roman Urdu output, and an LLM post-pass that restores English technical terms Whisper rendered in Urdu script. No commercial product markets this, and the only comparable work is CLE Pakistan's research on Urdu meeting minutes ([CLE](https://cle.org.pk/research/projects)). It costs nothing beyond one extra LLM pass. Its weakness as a *standalone* headline is that Fireflies already handles Urdu and multi-language meetings at the transcription level ([Fireflies Guide](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/2973706448-learn-about-fireflies-supported-languages)), so a panel can easily see it as "a better Urdu mode." That is why the recommendation folds it *into* the Ledger as the code-mixed input and Roman Urdu toggle rather than leading with it.

**Runner-up 2: "Anonymous AI Devil's Advocate."** During a live Stream call, a junior participant privately submits a concern, and an LLM paraphrases it and posts it to the call as the AI's own question, which protects the author's identity. This implements an IUI 2025 proposal that has **no quantitative results yet** ([arXiv 2502.06251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06251)), and no mainstream tool offers it. It is cheap: a single LLM call per concern, broadcast with the installed SDK's `sendCustomEvent`, which is confirmed in the `@stream-io/video-client` 1.57.0 type definitions. It is the most *original* option and makes a vivid live demo, especially for student-supervisor or viva power dynamics. It ranks second because its value is harder to measure in a small study, it touches live Stream code that has caused bugs before (so the Stream SDK verification rule applies), and the custom-event payload and rate limits are unverified.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "what AI feature is missing?" to "what does no one check *after* the AI has written its notes?" Every competitor stops at producing artifacts such as summaries and action lists. Nobody closes the loop by holding those artifacts against what happens next. NeuraMeet's advantage is not a better model, because it has the same free models as everyone else. Its advantage is that it already owns the whole chain on one free stack: native, consented recording, Urdu-capable transcription, user-scoped pgvector, and a follow-ups system. Very little new infrastructure turns that chain into memory.

For the FYP specifically, the Ledger converts a feature into a research contribution. Cross-meeting reversal and commitment-fulfilment detection has, per these notes, no controlled evaluation in the literature, and certainly none on Urdu-English speech. A modest, honestly reported study with precision and recall split by language, and a task-level user study, is therefore new evidence rather than a demo. If time runs short, the scope can shrink to commitments only (L1 to L5 without decisions) and still keep the core claim intact.
