# Take the Promise Ledger Live Mid-Call

NeuraMeet's second signature feature should be **Live Ledger**, which brings the Promise Ledger into the call itself. The pitch: *"While you are still in the meeting, NeuraMeet puts last week's promise on everyone's screen with the exact quote, lets the owner mark it kept or still open on the spot, and turns any new decision into an explicit one-tap agreement that goes straight into the ledger."* It has two parts. **Promise Check-in** is a ranked panel of the owner's open and overdue promises; the owner can "raise" one, and it appears as a quote card for every participant. **Agreement Check** lets the host state a decision, everyone taps Agree or Not yet, and the result is saved as a ledger decision tagged "agreed by 3 of 4". Live Ledger beats the other candidates for three reasons. First, live in-call AI has mostly become standard: on-demand Q&A, live summaries and agenda nudges ship from Zoom, Meet, Teams, Granola and Fathom. Second, none of the products reviewed pushes a *past promise with its quote* into the call mid-meeting, and none runs an AI-logged consensus check. Third, almost all of it already exists in the codebase: the ledger tables, the `match_ledger_prep_items` RPC and the custom-event channel the guest-admission flow already uses in production. The core costs about **one embedding call per meeting and no Whisper or Supabase Realtime usage**. The honest limits are these. The core version is triggered by the host, not by speech. Fully automatic, speech-triggered cards depend on live Urdu recognition that neither Stream's free tier nor Chrome reliably provides, so they are a stretch goal. And a start-of-meeting recap of past decisions already exists in one vendor's claims (InterMIND's Mia), so the uniqueness claim has to rest on the mid-call, quote-backed, owner-confirmed loop and on the logged agreement check.

## Most live AI features are already standard, but two gaps remain

The live-AI market changed fast in 2025–2026, and most ideas a student would propose for an "in-call AI" feature are already taken. Granola runs a chat agent before, during and after meetings that searches months of notes with inline citations, but only when the user asks ([Granola help](https://help.granola.ai/article/chatting-with-your-meetings-ask-granola)). Fathom 3.0 added live summaries during the meeting and a library-wide "Ask Fathom" with citations in April 2026 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260415965820/en/)). Zoom AI Companion 3.0 advertises "proactive meeting recommendations" that nudge teams to stay on the agenda ([UC Today](https://uctoday.com/top-10-new-transformative-zoom-ai-companion-features-for-it-leaders)). A competitor review says Zoom's documentation shows only preset prompts such as "Catch me up" and describes no unprompted actions ([InterMIND blog](https://intermind.com/blog/ai-meeting-assistant-in-the-meeting)), so Zoom's "proactive" is best read as agenda-level. Avoma pops up suggested answers during sales calls ([moge.ai](https://moge.ai/product/avoma)). Raddle's AI Facilitator on Webex already prompts groups and shows a live speaker-balance meter ([Webex App Hub](https://apphub.webex.com/applications/ai-meeting-facilitator-by-raddle-raddle-19041)). That last one rules out a silent-participant nudge as a headline.

Two gaps remain. The first is **evidence-backed recall that the system pushes into the call**, rather than answers on request. The second is **making agreement explicit**. The InterMIND review notes that none of Teams, Zoom, Meet or Mia documents "anonymous input, polls, or consensus checks during meetings" ([InterMIND blog](https://intermind.com/blog/ai-meeting-assistant-in-the-meeting)), and no product turned up that runs an AI-logged consensus check. The closest competitor is InterMIND's Mia, which by its own blog posts previous decisions and open items *at the start* of a session and auto-notes commitments live ([InterMIND blog](https://intermind.com/blog/ai-meeting-assistant-in-the-meeting)). Startups such as Proactor claim live cards when the conversation contradicts a past meeting ([Proactor](https://proactor.ai/blog/?p=3369)). Live Ledger therefore has to be framed against those two, not against the majors.

| Live Ledger component | Closest existing product | Claim status |
|---|---|---|
| Open and overdue promises shown at call start | InterMIND Mia posts previous decisions and open items at session start (vendor blog, not tested) | **Not unique.** Treat it as table stakes and cite Mia as related work |
| Answering "what did we promise last time?" when asked mid-call | Granola, Otter "Hey Otter", Fathom, Fellow, Gemini in Meet | **Verified commoditized.** Do not claim it |
| Pushing a past promise card mid-call, with the original quote and date, to every participant | Proactor and ContextGuard claim live *contradiction* cards. No product found that pushes *commitment* cards with quotes | **Unverified absence.** This is the core novelty claim |
| Owner marks the promise Kept, Still open or Reversed during the call, and the transcript evidence confirms it after the call | Not found | **Unverified absence** |
| AI-logged consensus check that writes "agreed by 3 of 4" into a decision ledger | Not found. InterMIND's review says no major tool documents consensus checks | **Unverified absence**, resting on a biased source |
| Works on code-mixed Urdu-English meetings | No product found. Urdu-script captions exist in Meet (paid) ([Meet Help](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/10964115)) | **Inherits the Promise Ledger's claim, with a caveat (see the risks section)** |

The defensible sentence for the report and the viva follows the narrow wording already in AGENTS.md: *"None of the major platforms or notetakers we reviewed push a past promise with its quote into a live call, let the owner confirm it on the spot, or log an explicit in-call agreement into a cross-meeting decision ledger."* Several entries in that review rest on snippets, aggregator pages or InterMIND's own competitor review, and Fathom 3.0 and Teams Facilitator documentation could not be fetched. The write-up should say "in our review" every time.

## Re-litigated decisions and false consensus are the needs it targets

The underlying pain is the one that justified the Promise Ledger. In Atlassian's survey of 5,000 knowledge workers, meetings were ineffective 72% of the time and **77% said meetings mainly create more meetings**, which Fortune summarised as "no decisions were made, and nobody knows what's expected of them" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/03/21/meetings-productivity-ineffective-atlassian-report)). The post-meeting ledger catches a broken promise *after* the next meeting has happened. Live Ledger catches it *during* that meeting, while the person who made the promise is present to answer. That is the moment when accountability is cheapest. This is the product logic for moving the ledger into the call. It is an inference: no user study of proactive mid-call memory cards was found.

The Agreement Check rests on stronger and more recent evidence. A 2026 study with 879 participants and real money at stake found that LLM facilitation "did not significantly improve group consensus" and created an **"illusion of inclusion"**: people rated the process as inclusive, but participation equity did not change ([arXiv 2605.14097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14097)). The design lesson is that an AI which only *facilitates* produces a false sense of agreement. Asking every participant to confirm a written decision does the opposite, because it turns assumed agreement into recorded agreement, including the "Not yet" votes. That this improves outcomes is an untested inference. It is, however, the kind of claim a small FYP study can test.

The Pakistani context adds a practical argument rather than a headline. Evening load shedding drains telecom-tower batteries and degrades internet at peak class hours, and 2026 reporting describes students missing online classes as a result ([CW Pakistan](https://cwpakistan.com/pakistan-internet-disruptions-worsen-as-evening-loadshedding-compounds-submarine-cable-maintenance-challenges/)). A participant who dropped out during the discussion still sees the decision card and casts a vote on rejoining. The agreement record then shows exactly who confirmed and who never did.

## The core fits the existing code, with Stream and data caveats

Live Ledger's core rests on three things NeuraMeet already has in production.

**Retrieval.** The `ledger_items` and `ledger_links` tables exist (`supabase/schema.sql:323-354`), with statuses open, kept and reversed, quote languages en, ur and mixed, and an embedding on every item. The RPC `match_ledger_prep_items(query_embedding, p_user_id, match_count)` already returns the owner's open items closest to an embedding. `findPrepLedgerItems` in `src/agent/prep.ts` already embeds text and calls that RPC, and `POST /api/prep` already returns the top five open or overdue `ledgerItems`. The Promise Check-in panel is therefore the existing prep query, seeded with the meeting's title and description and rendered in the room.

**Broadcast.** The installed `@stream-io/video-client` 1.57.0 declares `sendCustomEvent` ("Sends a custom event to all call participants") (`Call.d.ts:817`). `src/app/api/guest/request-join/route.ts` already sends server-side events through `StreamClient.video.sendCallEvent`, and `MeetingRoom.tsx:784` already listens with `call.on("custom", …)`. Stream documents a **5 KB cap per custom event** ([Stream custom events](https://getstream.io/video/docs/react/advanced/events/)), so a card carries only an item ID, the statement, the assignee and the due date, well under 1 KB.

**Language-model wrapper.** `generateStructuredWithFallback` (Gemini, falling back to Groq after a 30-second abort) handles the one optional call that rewrites a host's typed decision into a self-contained sentence.

The per-meeting cost of the core for a one-hour, five-person meeting is roughly **one embedding call, zero to a few LLM calls, zero Whisper seconds, zero Supabase Realtime messages, and 10 to 20 Stream custom events**. All of these are free-tier resources the project already uses. Stream's pricing lists participant minutes, not events, and no source says whether custom events count against any quota. Treat that as a small open question.

The four risks below decide how the feature is built.

**1. Who sent the event is visible.** A received `CustomVideoEvent` carries the sender's `user` field (`gen/coordinator/index.d.ts`). This is harmless for Live Ledger, because cards and votes are meant to be open. It is still a reason to send cards and tallies **server-side under a bot user** such as `neurameet-ai`. That lets the route enforce "owner only" (returning 403), save the event before broadcasting it, and give every card the same sender.

**2. The room component is fragile.** `MeetingRoom.tsx` is 1,100 lines, and its comments record that churn in `useParticipants()` after a camera toggle remounted the video tiles. The panel and the card overlay must be small, separate components outside the tile tree. They should listen to `call.on("custom")` and must not subscribe to participant arrays. The core touches no device code, so it avoids the camera-loop and `sfuClient` bug classes. The permanent AGENTS.md rule still applies: check the `stream-react` skill's `references/VIDEO.md` before writing the listener, and confirm that the room's call-type role has the `send-event` grant.

**3. Event rate limits are unpublished.** No source gives a limit for `sendCustomEvent` or `sendCallEvent`, so throttle each sender to at most one event per second.

**4. Live speech recognition is the real blocker, which is why it is a stretch goal.** Stream's closed captions are a paid add-on at $8 per 1,000 call minutes and do not support Urdu ([Stream pricing](https://getstream.io/video/pricing/)). Chrome's on-device speech recognition uses the same language packs as Live Caption, and **Urdu is not on that list**; Hindi is ([Chrome Help](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/10538231?hl=en)). Cloud-mode `ur-PK` in Chrome is plausible, because Google Cloud Speech supports it ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/dotnet/docs/reference/Google.Cloud.Speech.V1/latest/Google.Cloud.Speech.V1.LanguageCodes.Urdu)). Chrome does not document it, though, and one recognizer is locked to one language, so mixed Urdu-English speech will degrade. `SpeechRecognition.start(audioTrack)` could reuse Stream's own microphone track instead of opening a second capture, but MDN marks it "limited availability" ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechRecognition/start)). The alternative, sending audio chunks to Groq Whisper, uses about 3,600 of the 7,200 audio-seconds Groq allows per hour, enough for roughly one live meeting at a time. Automatic cards should therefore be built only after a five-minute runtime spike shows that `SpeechRecognition.available({langs:["ur-PK"], processLocally:false})` works.

Three data gaps also need design decisions:

- **No timestamps in stored transcripts.** Lines are stored as plain `Speaker: text`. `mergeSegmentsToTranscript` drops segment start times (`src/agent/transcript-generation.ts:202-281`), so a live tap cannot be tied to a transcript moment. The fix is for the post-call `/api/ledger/match` run to treat a live mark as a strong prior and attach the quote it finds in the transcript afterwards. In the ledger, a live tap is a *claim*, and the transcript later supplies the *evidence*.
- **Urdu quotes are translated away.** The cleanup prompt for generated transcripts requires that the "final output MUST be entirely in English" (`transcript-generation.ts:64`). In practice, `quote_language: ur|mixed` only fires on uploaded transcripts. Any "works in Urdu-English" claim for meetings recorded live needs that translation step made optional, or it should be scoped honestly to uploaded transcripts.
- **Talk time is measured as mic-on time.** `talk_time_seconds` counts time with the microphone enabled, not actual speech. This does not affect Live Ledger, but it is worth knowing before anyone proposes engagement-flavoured extensions.

## A six-step build that extends the ledger instead of duplicating it

The build follows the project workflow: `/architect` first, because the feature needs new routes and a small schema addition, and the AGENTS.md API contract must be amended before any code is written. The route names below are placeholders for S0-style decisions, not contract entries.

| Step | What it does | Reuses |
|---|---|---|
| LL0 `/architect live ledger` | Lock the routes (for example a GET for check-in items, a POST to raise a card, a POST to open or vote on an agreement). Lock the schema: a `ledger_live_marks` table (item_id, meeting_id, mark kept/open/reversed, marked_by, marked_at) and agreement columns or a table (decision text, votes, agreed count). Verify custom events, the `send-event` grant and the listener pattern against the `stream-react` skill. Add the routes to the API Contracts list | S0 spec, `docs/specs/0001-promise-ledger/` |
| LL1 Check-in route | Owner only. Embed the meeting title and description, call `match_ledger_prep_items`, and return the top open or overdue items with quotes and due dates. No LLM call | `findPrepLedgerItems`, `embedTexts`, `/api/prep` scoping |
| LL2 Owner panel in the room | A collapsible side panel, a separate component outside the tile tree, using `useLoadingTimeout` and `LoadError` and the design tokens. A "Raise" button on each item | Existing room shell, UI rules |
| LL3 Raise card and live mark | The server checks ownership, saves the raise, and calls `sendCallEvent` under the bot user with `{event:"ledger.card", itemId, statement, assignee, dueDate}`. Every client renders a dismissible quote card. The owner taps Kept, Still open or Reversed, which writes a `ledger_live_marks` row and broadcasts the result | Guest-flow server send, `call.on("custom")` listener at `MeetingRoom.tsx:784` |
| LL4 Agreement Check | The host types a decision. An optional `generateStructuredWithFallback` call rewrites it as a self-contained sentence. The server broadcasts it, participants vote Agree or Not yet through the route, and the server tallies the votes and broadcasts the count. When the check closes, a `ledger_items` row is written with kind `decision`, the agreed and total counts, and the source meeting | Ledger schema, LLM wrapper, Clerk participant check |
| LL5 Post-call reconcile | Extend `/api/ledger/match` so that live marks act as priors. When the transcript supports a mark, attach the quote and line and set the link to `confirmed`. When it does not, show "marked live, no transcript evidence found" for owner review. The Ledger tab gets a "Marked live" badge | `/api/ledger/match`, `/api/ledger/links/[id]/review` |
| LL6 Stretch: speech trigger | Only after the `ur-PK` spike passes. Each client runs Web Speech on its own mic track, sends text windows (not audio) to a route every 60 seconds, and a card is suggested to the owner when similarity clears a threshold. The owner still approves before anything is broadcast | `embedTexts`, `match_ledger_prep_items` |

Steps LL1 to LL5 form the demoable core, at roughly five steps of student work. If time runs short, drop LL4 and keep the promise loop. The feature story survives, but the consensus-check novelty is lost.

## A four-minute demo, an evaluation, and the risks the panel will raise

The demo should never depend on speech recognition. Use two seeded meetings from the existing Promise Ledger demo, then run one live call with two browsers (the owner, and a "member" in an incognito window).

| Time | Action | What the panel sees |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Framing: "Last month we showed the Ledger remembers promises after the meeting. Live Ledger brings that into the meeting itself, while the person who promised is still there." | The Ledger tab of seeded Meeting 2, with Ali's survey promise marked **Overdue** |
| 0:30–1:30 | Start Meeting 3 as the owner and open the Live Ledger panel | Ranked open items, each with its original code-mixed quote ("main Friday tak survey results share kar doon ga") and date |
| 1:30–2:30 | Tap **Raise** on Ali's promise | A card slides in on *both* screens: "Ali, 24 Oct: '…', 3 days overdue." Ali says, "survey bhej diya tha". The owner taps **Kept**, and the panel updates on both screens |
| 2:30–3:30 | Host types "We deploy on Vercel free tier, no paid hosting" and starts an **Agreement Check** | The member's screen shows Agree / Not yet. The member taps Not yet and the tally reads "1 of 2 agreed". The host discusses, the member switches to Agree, and the decision is saved as "agreed by 2 of 2" |
| 3:30–4:30 | End the call and run generation and match (or upload a prepared transcript of the same call to save time) | The Ledger tab shows Ali's item as **Kept**, with a "Marked live" badge and the transcript quote attached, plus the new agreed decision ready for checking in Meeting 4 |

The evaluation should mirror the Promise Ledger's two-layer design and claim task-level gains only. A preregistered field experiment across 7,196 meetings found that goal nudges did **not** significantly improve meeting effectiveness ([arXiv 2602.16939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16939)), and a panel that knows this literature will respect restraint.

**Layer one is technical.** First, measure **precision at five** of the check-in panel: of the top five items shown for a meeting, how many were relevant to that meeting's agenda, as judged by two independent labellers with Cohen's kappa reported. Second, measure **live-mark agreement**: how often the post-call transcript match confirms the owner's live tap, reported separately for English and code-mixed meetings.

**Layer two is a small within-subjects study** with 6 to 10 participants in mock FYP-group meetings. One condition uses prep suggestions only, the other uses Live Ledger. Measure the number of overdue promises actually raised and resolved during the call, and the share of decisions where a post-meeting private survey shows that participants disagree about what was decided. A drop in that disagreement is a direct test of the "illusion of inclusion" problem. Add SUS and a trust rating.

The panel will probe five risks:

- **The core is not "AI noticing" anything.** The host chooses what to raise from an AI-ranked list. Present this as deliberate human control: the system recalls and ranks, and the human decides when to interrupt. The speech trigger remains a measured stretch goal rather than a promise.
- **Public cards can embarrass people.** Showing "3 days overdue" to the whole call is socially loaded. Default the card wording to "Still open?" rather than "Overdue", and let the owner preview a card privately before broadcasting it.
- **The Urdu claim is weaker for live-recorded meetings** while the English-only cleanup step stays in place, as described above.
- **Novelty can be narrowed by tools not tested hands-on.** InterMIND, Proactor and ContextGuard were assessed only from vendor claims. Cite them openly as related work.
- **Anonymity questions.** If a panellist asks "why not anonymous?", the answer is evidence-based. A CSCW 2026 study (N=96) found that the **anonymous AI-mediated message condition lowered junior members' psychological safety** to M=3.17, against 4.25 at baseline (β=−1.40, p<.001), and cut their satisfaction to 2.92. Openly AI-generated counterarguments scored 4.92 on satisfaction ([arXiv 2606.31762](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31762v1)). Live Ledger's open, attributed agreement is consistent with that finding. The study's limits are a Korean sample, role-played hierarchy and text chat rather than video, so cite it as a caution, not a law.

## Two runners-up kept in reserve

**Runner-up 1: "While You Were Gone", a recap scoped to each person's dropout window.** This has the strongest *local* evidence of any candidate. Evening load shedding degrades mobile internet ([CW Pakistan](https://cwpakistan.com/pakistan-internet-disruptions-worsen-as-evening-loadshedding-compounds-submarine-cable-maintenance-challenges/)), and regional suspensions kept students out of virtual classes for weeks, including nearly two months in Lower South Waziristan ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/pakistan-lower-south-waziristan-residents-facing-hardships-due-to-prolonged-internet-suspension--20260617115906)). Competitors offer only meeting-level catch-up: Teams Copilot's "Recap the meeting" is generic and paid ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/meetings-events/recap-a-teams-meeting)). `attendance_events` already records the exact gap between sessions. The feature ranks second because of the data gap: transcripts carry no timestamps, so it needs segment times persisted in a new column first. Uploaded transcripts will never have timings, and a live version needs the same unverified live speech recognition. It would pair well with Live Ledger later, for example "promises made while you were away".

**Runner-up 2: Viva / FYP Panel Mode.** This would build a per-question record of a viva from the transcript: each examiner question, the student's answer with quotes, talk share per question, and a "required changes" list that becomes ledger items checked at the next supervisor meeting. The panel would relate to it personally, and it is zero-cost reuse of the ledger. Proctoring suites only target written-exam cheating, and no meeting tool was found that records a viva this way. It ranks below Live Ledger because it is an education mode of the existing ledger rather than a new capability, and integrity heuristics such as "answer read aloud" are risky to present as accusations.

Three ideas were dropped. Roman Urdu output is a near-free language layer worth adding to everything, but it is too easy to copy to be a headline. The silent-participant nudge already exists in niche tools such as Raddle. The anonymous devil's advocate, a runner-up in the first report, now has direct negative evidence from CSCW 2026.

## Conclusion

The research moves NeuraMeet's story from "it remembers" to **"it remembers, it brings that memory into the room, and it makes agreement explicit"**. That arc is hard to copy because each step depends on the one before: no competitor reviewed has a quote-backed promise ledger to push into a call in the first place. The less obvious finding is that the feature most worth building is also the *least* AI-heavy. Its novelty comes from what it connects (pgvector recall, a server-sent Stream event, a human tap, and transcript evidence after the call), not from a new model. That is exactly why it fits a zero-cost stack.

The strategic implication is to keep live speech recognition off the critical path. Every failure mode that has hurt this project before, including device bugs, Stream version behavior and Urdu recognition, sits in that layer. The core can be demoed, measured and defended without it, and a passing `ur-PK` spike can turn the speech trigger from future work into a bonus a week before the viva.
