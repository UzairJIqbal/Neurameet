# Prove the Promise From Quote to Merge

The "Conversations to Code" workspace in the PDF mostly exists already, in pieces: Huly ships a free workspace with built-in video, live transcription, an issue tracker and two-way GitHub sync; Atlassian, Microsoft and a dozen notetakers turn meetings into tracker tickets, some with human review; and Linear, Jira, Huly and Notion all move a task to Done when its PR merges. What none of the reviewed products does is the one thing the PDF gestures at but never names: **carry a verbatim meeting quote, speaker and timestamp through a human-reviewed task and a GitHub PR to a merge on the default branch, and show that evidence back on the meeting and on the promise across later meetings**. That narrow chain is genuinely new, and NeuraMeet is unusually well placed to build it, because its Promise Ledger already stores quotes, speakers and kept/open/overdue/reversed status and already links ledger items to action items. As drawn, though, the PDF is a broad Jira + Zoom + GitHub clone with 21 screens, three critical security holes (an unverified webhook, cross-project data leakage through service-role routes, and leakage through vector search), a "merged → Completed" rule that produces false completions, a live-captions promise that breaks the zero-cost rule and has no Urdu support on Stream, and no mention of the three signature features that are the project's actual differentiation. The recommended completion is a **project workspace whose core loop is "meeting → quote-checked proposals → human review → task → linked PR → evidence-based completion → ledger marked kept"**, built on app-level projects in Supabase (not Clerk Organizations), a GitHub App with strict linking rules, post-meeting Groq Whisper transcripts instead of live captions, and the Promise Ledger, Live Ledger and Tasdeeq folded in as the Decisions view, the in-call panel and the minutes sign-off step. It is achievable on the approved free tiers, but it is roughly 9 to 12 developer-weeks of work, so it needs four explicit decisions from the team before any code is written: lift the "multi-tenant out of scope" line, choose between the locked Stitch tokens and the PDF palette, drop or downgrade live captions, and confirm the signature features stay.

## Every screen in the PDF already ships somewhere, but nobody closes the loop

The closest single product is **Huly**, an open-source "everything app" with issue tracking, chat, video calls and docs in one workspace ([openalternative](https://openalternative.co/huly)), a LiveKit-based virtual office with recording and live transcription in 40+ languages ([openapps.pro](https://openapps.pro/apps/huly)), and bidirectional GitHub sync in which merging a PR flips the issue status automatically ([toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/huly)). Its free Common plan allows unlimited users, 10 GB storage and 10 GB of video/audio traffic ([dupple](https://www.dupple.com/tools/hulyio)). Huly's gap is the AI half: its own documentation says the Hulia assistant is **"limited to transcription"** and will only eventually summarize meetings and create action items ([Huly docs](https://docs.huly.io/communication/live-transcription/)). That page is undated, so Huly may have shipped more since, and the report treats this as the biggest uncertainty in the competitive picture.

The "meeting → AI tasks → human review" half is crowded. Atlassian's Loom meetings connected to a Jira space produce "a batch of suggested updates" that Rovo maps from the transcript to existing work items for human review ([Atlassian support](https://support.atlassian.com/ja/loom/docs/get-ai-suggested-work-item-updates-from-loom-meetings-in-jira/)), but only on Jira Standard or above with Rovo and Loom AI enabled ([Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/loom/turn-meetings-and-bugs-into-action)). Teams' Facilitator agent captures decisions as tasks during the meeting and syncs them to Planner ([Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft365insiderblog/keep-meetings-focused-and-productive-with-facilitator-in-teams/4456029)). Google Meet's "take notes for me" assigns owners and suggests due dates that users edit ([Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/02/google-meet-take-notes-for-me-next-steps.html)). Fireflies pushes action items to Jira after every meeting ([Fireflies](https://fireflies.ai/integrations/project-management/jira)), Spinach holds ticket drafts for approval ([Spinach](https://www.spinach.ai/content/ticket-management)), and Supernormal drafts Linear issues with acceptance criteria ([Supernormal](https://www.supernormal.com/integrations/linear)). "Nothing is created without human review" (PDF p.20) is therefore good practice, not novelty.

The "tasks → code" half is commodity too. Linear moves an issue to Done when its linked PR merges ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/de/blog/linear-ai)), Notion has a PR property that updates task status ([Notion 2.30](https://notion.com/releases/2023-05-31)), and Plane offers bidirectional GitHub sync free for up to 12 seats ([everydev](https://www.everydev.ai/tools/plane)). Linear, the obvious benchmark, has no native meeting capture at all and relies on Granola, Read AI, Fellow and Tactiq integrations ([Linear: Granola](https://linear.app/integrations/granola); [Linear: Read AI](https://linear.app/integrations/read-ai)).

A student team cannot out-build Huly on workspace breadth or Atlassian on tracker depth, and an examiner who has used either will rate a 21-screen clone as weaker. The defensible ground is the column where NeuraMeet already leads, the decisions log, extended into a chain nobody else draws. Pakistani software houses run on global tools such as Jira and ClickUp ([TheirStack Jira PK](https://theirstack.com/en/technology/jira/pk); [TheirStack ClickUp PK](https://theirstack.com/en/technology/clickup/pk)), and no local integrated meeting-plus-code workspace was found, so there is no home-grown incumbent either.

## The genuinely new part is a promise you can follow to the merge commit

No product reviewed presents a single record that says, in effect, "this commitment, said by Ali at 12:04 in the sprint meeting, became task NM-42, was delivered by PR #51 merged to main, and the promise is now marked kept." Rovo links meeting decisions to existing Jira items, which is meeting → item, not code → meeting quote ([Atlassian support](https://support.atlassian.com/ja/loom/docs/get-ai-suggested-work-item-updates-from-loom-meetings-in-jira/)). The nearest concept matches are an enterprise compliance pitch, VDF AI's "decision traceability map" across stories, PRs and meetings ([vdf.ai](https://vdf.ai/use-cases/decision-traceability-map-audits/)), and a student prototype, DecisionTrace, which extracts decisions with confirm/edit/reject but has no transcripts or GitHub linking ([GitHub](https://github.com/wyz-water/DecisionTrace)). The research literature supports the problem without solving it: an ICSE 2024 study characterizes what software maintenance meetings share and decide ([ICSE 2024](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2024/icse-2024-research-track/48/Characterizing-Software-Maintenance-Meetings-Information-Shared-Discussion-Outcomes)), UCI's "Land of Lost Knowledge" finds unlinked commits hold **5 to 21% of the knowledge needed for future modification** ([UCI](https://ics.uci.edu/~iftekha/publication/land-of-lost-knowledge-an-initial-investigation-into-projects-lost-knowledge)), and spoken design rationale from meetings usually evaporates ([UCI workshop paper](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~iftekha/pdf/W.7.pdf)). No paper found traces spoken meeting commitments to commits or PRs end to end, which gives the FYP report a citable research gap.

Three things are new in combination, and the claim should stay as narrow as the Promise Ledger's ("none of the products we reviewed"). First, **quote-anchored provenance carried end to end**: every task keeps the verbatim quote, speaker and timestamp it came from, checked by code to exist in the transcript, all the way to the PR. Second, **"verified done" as evidence, not a status flip**: a merge counts only under strict rules, and the evidence (PR number, merge commit, target branch) is visible from the meeting view. Third, **cross-meeting promise status that accepts code as one source of "kept"**: the Promise Ledger already marks promises kept, open, overdue or reversed from later transcripts, and a qualifying merge becomes another kind of evidence alongside the quote from a later meeting. Bilingual Urdu-English handling, already in NeuraMeet's Groq Whisper pipeline, is a local edge that no global competitor markets.

What must not be claimed as new: built-in video plus tracker plus GitHub sync (Huly), meeting-to-tickets (many), human review of AI tasks (Atlassian, Spinach, Teams), PR merge → Done (everyone), workspace roles and invites (everyone), and RAG over meetings (most notetakers). The window is also short. Atlassian Rovo, Linear's agent work and Huly's announced "MetaBrain" knowledge base ([bestaitools](https://www.bestaitools.com/tool/huly/)) are each one integration away from the same loop, so this is a strong FYP and demo differentiator, not a durable moat.

## Three critical loopholes and a false-completion rule must be fixed before any demo

The PDF's biggest risks are not AI risks; they are plain access-control and automation bugs that would be cheap to prevent and embarrassing in a viva.

### The webhook, the routes and the vector search can all leak or lie

GitHub signs each webhook with an HMAC SHA-256 digest in `X-Hub-Signature-256`, and its docs say to compare with `crypto.timingSafeEqual`, never `==` ([GitHub Docs: validating deliveries](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/using-webhooks/validating-webhook-deliveries)). If NeuraMeet's endpoint skips this, anyone who knows the URL can POST a fake `pull_request` "merged" payload and complete any task. In a Next.js route handler the signature must be checked over `await req.text()` before parsing JSON. GitHub also expects a 2xx response within **10 seconds**, recommends deduplicating on the unique `X-GitHub-Delivery` header, and notes that redeliveries keep the original ID ([GitHub Docs: best practices](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/using-webhooks/best-practices-for-using-webhooks)). Because **"GitHub does not automatically redeliver failed deliveries"** ([GitHub Docs: failed deliveries](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/using-webhooks/handling-failed-webhook-deliveries)), a Vercel cold start can silently drop a merge, so the PDF's "Sync GitHub" button must run a real reconciliation that re-reads linked PR state.

The second critical hole is structural. NeuraMeet reads and writes Supabase through the service-role key in API routes, and Supabase's docs warn that a secret key bypasses RLS ([Supabase RLS](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security)). Today every authorization check is hand-written per route against `meetings.user_id` or `attendance_events` (`src/app/api/meetings/[id]/route.ts`), and about 29 route files compare `user_id` directly. Once projects exist, one missed membership check exposes another team's meetings, transcripts or tasks, which breaks the PDF's own promise that "project data never mixes" (PDF p.2). The fix is a single `requireProjectRole(userId, projectId, minRole)` helper used by every project-scoped route, resolving the project from the record rather than from the request, plus a test per route that calls it as a non-member and expects 403 or 404. The third hole is the same bug in a quieter place: the existing `match_meeting_chunks_user` RPC filters by `m.user_id` (`supabase/schema.sql`), and a project assistant must filter by project membership **inside the SQL**, not after top-k retrieval, or other teams' chunks reach the prompt and the citations.

### "Completed on merge" marks unfinished work as done

The PDF says webhooks "move tasks to In Review on PR open and to Completed on merge" (PDF p.2). Taken literally, that closes a task on a partial PR, closes every task a PR mentions, closes on a merge into `develop`, and on a PR that was closed without merging, since GitHub sends `closed` for both. GitHub itself only auto-closes issues from closing keywords ("closes", "fixes", "resolves" and variants) in PRs that target the **default branch**, and ignores keywords on other branches ([GitHub Docs: linking a PR](https://docs.github.com/en/issues/tracking-your-work-with-issues/using-issues/linking-a-pull-request-to-an-issue)). Linear adds non-closing words ("refs", "part of", "contributes to") that link without changing status, and when several PRs touch one issue it updates status **only when the final PR reaches the required state** ([Linear Docs: GitHub](https://linear.app/docs/github)). NeuraMeet should copy that rule set and add what neither documents: a draft PR stays In Progress until `ready_for_review`, an unmerged close sends the task back, and a revert PR reopens the task with an activity entry. Revert detection is heuristic, because the "Reverts owner/repo#51" body is an editable UI template. Linking should use NeuraMeet task keys such as `NM-42` in branch names and PR text, because many tasks will never have a GitHub issue. Evidence should store the PR number and merge commit SHA, not branch-head SHAs that a force push can erase. Finally, the "development simulator" on Task Details (PDF p.13) shows a fake issue → commit → PR chain next to real evidence; in a viva it will read as faked automation and should be removed or loudly labelled.

### Identity, invites and roles need written rules

The Members screen lists a "GitHub username" per member (PDF p.14). If that is typed text, a member can claim a teammate's handle; commit author emails are equally spoofable, which is why GitHub shows "Verified" only for signed commits ([GitHub Docs: signature verification](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/managing-commit-signature-verification/about-commit-signature-verification)). The handle should come only from an OAuth "Connect GitHub" flow, stored as the immutable numeric GitHub user ID, with work attributed by PR opener and merger, which GitHub authenticates. Invite links need at least 128 bits of CSPRNG entropy, a SHA-256 hash at rest, server-enforced expiry, single use for email invites bound to the accepting user's verified email, and owner approval **on by default** for shareable links. Role escalation must be closed in code: only an Owner promotes to Admin, nobody edits their own role, the last Owner cannot leave, and an invite's role is fixed when it is created. The PDF lists a Viewer role but never says what Viewers see; the safe default is summaries, decisions and tasks, with no recordings, raw transcripts or private-repo data unless the Owner allows it. Member removal, an append-only audit log for role and GitHub changes, and a per-project retention setting all need one line each in `context/architecture.md`.

### The AI must propose with evidence, not decide

A 2026 study of LLM conversation summarization found meeting scenarios have **the worst faithfulness**, and that reasoning models give "no gain, or even regress" ([arXiv:2606.15974](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15974)). The PDF reviews proposed tasks but appears to publish Decisions (PDF p.23) without review, and a hallucinated decision looks more authoritative than a hallucinated task. Every proposed task and decision should carry a quote that code verifies against the transcript, which is exactly what the Promise Ledger's validator already does; items whose quote is not found are dropped. The PDF's rule that assignees are suggested "only when someone clearly accepted the work" is right, and it can be enforced by requiring the acceptance quote to come from that person's own audio track (NeuraMeet transcribes per-participant tracks) or from a speaker label claimed through Tasdeeq, so "Ali will do it" said by Sara stays Unassigned. PR bodies and commit messages are written by anyone with push access, which is the indirect prompt-injection case OWASP describes ([OWASP LLM01](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)); the assistant should be read-only, wrap retrieved text as untrusted data, build its citation list in code from retrieved record IDs, and never let model output change a task status. Since an LLM judge correlates best with human faithfulness ratings among reviewed metrics ([arXiv:2501.00269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00269)), a cheap second-pass support check on assistant answers is a reasonable stretch.

### Consent and surveillance are the adoption risks

Pakistan has **no comprehensive data protection law** as of May 2026; the Personal Data Protection Bill is still pending ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=PK)). That removes the legal floor but not the ethics question, and foreign clients bring GDPR's lawful-basis and notice rules ([GDPR Art. 6](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-6-gdpr/); [GDPR Art. 13](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-13-gdpr/)). The live meeting shows a recording timer (PDF p.18) but no screen asks for consent, so a pre-join notice ("recorded, transcribed, AI will propose tasks") for members and guests is required. The dashboard's personal GitHub contributions, per-member task counts and member filters on the activity feed (PDF p.8, p.14, p.22) drift toward a productivity scoreboard; in JetBrains' 2025 survey of 24,534 developers, **66% said current metrics do not reflect their true contributions** ([JetBrains Research](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2025/10/state-of-developer-ecosystem-2025/)). Contributions should be visible only to the developer, with team-level throughput for managers and no rankings.

## Zero cost holds, provided live captions leave the core

Every core component fits the approved free tiers, and the one that does not is the PDF's live captions.

**GitHub** is free: GitHub's docs prefer GitHub Apps over OAuth apps for fine-grained permissions, installer-chosen repositories, centralized webhooks and one-hour installation tokens ([GitHub Docs: Apps vs OAuth](https://docs.github.com/en/apps/oauth-apps/building-oauth-apps/differences-between-github-apps-and-oauth-apps)). Installation tokens get 5,000 requests/hour, with a secondary limit of 80 content-creating requests per minute that matters only for bulk "create issue" ([GitHub Docs: rate limits](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api)). Local testing goes through smee.io ([GitHub Docs: testing webhooks](https://docs.github.com/webhooks/testing-and-troubleshooting-webhooks/testing-webhooks)). The App private key and webhook secret join the AGENTS.md "never expose" list.

**Vercel Hobby** allows a **300-second** maximum function duration, 1M invocations and 4 hours of active CPU per month, and **cron at most once a day** ([Vercel Limits](https://vercel.com/docs/limits); [Vercel Cron](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/usage-and-pricing)). Next.js `after()`, stable since 15.1 and backed by `waitUntil` on Vercel, lets the webhook route verify, insert the delivery row, return 202, and process afterwards within that budget ([Next.js docs: after](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/functions/after)), so no external queue is needed; the stored delivery row doubles as the retry record. Hobby is restricted to non-commercial use ([Vercel Fair Use](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)), which an FYP satisfies.

**Supabase Free** gives 500 MB of database, 1 GB storage, 200 concurrent Realtime connections and 2M Realtime messages a month, and pauses projects after a week idle ([Supabase Pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Realtime Broadcast suits Kanban moves ([Supabase Realtime limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/limits)). Clerk is now a native Supabase third-party auth provider, so RLS can use `auth.jwt()->>'sub'` joined to a `project_members` table if the browser ever reads directly ([Clerk Docs: Supabase](https://clerk.com/docs/guides/development/integrations/databases/supabase)). The 500 MB database, not compute, is the binding limit once embeddings grow.

**Clerk Organizations** are the wrong tool here even though they are free: the Hobby plan includes Organizations but caps them at **20 members, only Admin and Member roles**, with custom roles behind a $100/month add-on ([Clerk Pricing](https://clerk.com/pricing)). An org is also a tenant the user switches into, which clashes with the PDF's "My Dashboard across all projects". App-level `projects`, `project_members` and `project_invitations` tables in Supabase cost nothing, support the PDF's four roles, and keep Supabase the single source of truth.

**Resend Free** sends 3,000 emails a month and 100 a day ([Resend limits](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/account-quotas-and-limits)), but the `resend.dev` sender can only reach the account owner's own address ([Resend: 403 error](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/403-error-resend-dev-domain)). Invitations to teammates therefore need a verified domain the team controls; the copyable invite link must work on its own so email stays optional.

**Gemini and Groq** carry the extra AI load if calls are batched per meeting and made on demand. Google no longer publishes free-tier numbers outside AI Studio ([Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/rate-limits)), so the team must read its own key's limits. Groq's free table lists gpt-oss and Qwen models at 30 RPM, 1K requests/day and only **8K tokens/minute**, and no Llama 3.x model ([Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)), so Groq stays a fallback for short prompts and any Llama reference in `src/agent/models.ts` should be rechecked.

**Live captions** are where the PDF breaks the rule. Stream's free Build plan includes a $100 monthly credit and recording, but **transcription and closed captions are an $8 per 1,000 call-minute add-on** ([Stream Video pricing](https://getstream.io/video/pricing/)), and the installed SDK's caption language enum has **no Urdu** (`node_modules/@stream-io/video-client/dist/src/gen/coordinator/index.d.ts`). Whether captions draw from the free credit without a card was not confirmed. Groq Whisper's free tier allows 20 requests/minute, **7,200 audio seconds per hour and 28,800 per day** ([Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)): five-second live chunks need about 12 requests a minute per speaker, so the cap serves one or two speakers, and a four-person hour of per-track audio already consumes half the daily quota that post-call transcripts need. The only zero-cost live option is the browser Web Speech API, which in Chrome sends audio to Google's servers ([Chrome Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/voice-driven-web-apps-introduction-to-the-web-speech-api)), is absent from Firefox, and has on-device language packs (Edge lists six) with no Urdu ([Microsoft Edge docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/web-platform/speech-recognition-api)). Google's cloud recognizer does define `ur-PK` ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/dotnet/docs/reference/Google.Cloud.Speech.V1/latest/Google.Cloud.Speech.V1.LanguageCodes.Urdu)), but quality on code-mixed speech is untested.

The two new libraries are small and free: `@hello-pangea/dnd` 18.0.1 (React 19 peer, the maintained fork of react-beautiful-dnd) for the Kanban ([Puck](https://next.puckeditor.com/blog/top-5-drag-and-drop-libraries-for-react)), and `cmdk` 1.1.1 for the Ctrl K palette, backed by Postgres full-text plus the existing pgvector `/api/search`, using the `simple` text-search config so Roman Urdu is not stemmed away.

## The codebase already holds the meeting half of the PDF

A screen-by-screen comparison against the codebase finds about **4 of 21 screens built** (auth, live meeting, meeting AI summary, action items), about 4 partial (dashboard, settings, meetings list, AI assistant) and 13 missing, all because of five absent concepts: projects, members and invitations, a task lifecycle, GitHub, and notifications. The meeting engine is reusable as is: Stream wrappers and guest flow, attendance tracking and PDF, Groq Whisper transcript generation, `/api/ai/process` and embeddings, RAG, ledger matching, minutes, prep and Resend email (`src/agent/`, `src/app/api/`). The codebase is about 43,000 lines with 41 API routes and 555 passing vitest tests at Step T5 (`context/progress-tracker.md`).

The data model extends cleanly because every child table already cascades from `meetings`. A nullable `meetings.project_id` scopes transcripts, chunks, insights, ledger, minutes and attendance through one join. `action_items` becomes the "AI proposed" staging layer by adding `review_state`, priority, an evidence quote and a suggested assignee user ID; a new `tasks` table (with keys such as `NM-42`, five statuses, a real assignee user ID and a position column) supersedes `follow_ups`, which today have only three statuses, free-text assignees and no review gate (`supabase/schema.sql`). Decisions need no new table: `ledger_items` already has `kind = 'decision'`, quotes, speakers, line numbers, embeddings and an `action_item_id` link, which is the hook that joins a promise to its task. New tables are limited to projects, members, invitations, meeting participants, tasks and task events, GitHub installations, repos, deliveries and task links, notifications and an audit log; the activity timeline can be a SQL view over existing tables.

Three conflicts need care. First, ownership: every owner-only route (guest admit, attendance report, deletes, ledger match and review, live check-in and agreement, minutes review and seal) must map "owner" to a project role, Owner or Admin. Second, today's process route auto-creates follow-ups, which contradicts the PDF's review gate, and it is also locked with a 409 while a Tasdeeq review exists, so task confirmation must never re-run processing. Third, the design tokens: the app uses the locked Stitch palette (`primary #6500C4`, violet `#830EF9`) with a dark shell, while the PDF is all-light with a white sidebar, deep indigo primary, blue secondary and green/amber/red status colours, and gives no hex values. Because 63 components use semantic tokens, a palette swap is mostly `tailwind.config.ts` values plus about 10 files that hard-wire `inverse-surface`, `src/tailwind-config.test.ts`, and rewriting `context/ui-tokens.md`; the live meeting stays dark in both designs. Either way the token set needs success and warning colours it currently lacks.

Two housekeeping facts matter before any new work. The Live Ledger and Tasdeeq code is **entirely untracked in git**, alongside a 33-file uncommitted diff on tracked files (`git status`), and Live Ledger's LL5 live verify plus Tasdeeq's three-person run and planted-error study are still owed (`context/progress-tracker.md`). A pivot of this size on top of uncommitted work is how projects lose a month.

## The completed concept: a project workspace where promises carry proof

The recommended product keeps the PDF's information architecture (My Dashboard, My Tasks, My Meetings, My Projects, Notifications, project workspaces with Overview, Meetings, Tasks, Decisions, GitHub, Activity, Assistant, Members, Settings) but rebuilds its centre of gravity around one loop that works end to end and securely, which this report calls the **Proof Chain**:

A project meeting is recorded with visible consent and transcribed after the call by the existing per-track Groq Whisper pipeline in English, Urdu or both. `/api/ai/process` proposes tasks and decisions, each with a code-verified quote, speaker and timestamp, a suggested assignee only when that person's own track contains the acceptance, and a pgvector duplicate check against open project tasks ("possible duplicate of NM-42: link instead"). Optionally, Tasdeeq runs first, so attendees confirm or correct the lines attributed to them; their speaker claims become the verified speaker-to-user mapping the assignee rule needs. The owner or an admin confirms proposals into tasks on the Kanban, with an optional "also create GitHub issue". Developers reference `NM-42` in branch names and PRs; the GitHub App webhook applies strict rules (closing word, `merged == true`, base is the default branch, all linked PRs done, drafts and reverts handled) and records the PR and merge commit as evidence. The task completes, the linked promise in the ledger gains a "code" evidence link and moves to kept (owner-confirmed, matching the ledger's existing confirm/reject flow), and the meeting's Outcome card shows the chain. Tasks without GitHub complete manually with a comment or link as evidence, so the loop works for teams that never connect a repo. The project assistant answers over meetings, ledger items and tasks with citations built by code from retrieved record IDs, and says "not found in this project's records" when retrieval comes back empty.

The three signature features each get a permanent home rather than competing with the pivot:

| Signature feature | Status | Place in the workspace | Change needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Promise Ledger** | Built and merged (PR #12) | The project **Decisions & Promises** tab, replacing the PDF's plain decisions list; promises link to tasks via `ledger_items.action_item_id`; a qualifying merge becomes "kept" evidence | Scope queries by project instead of owner; add a `code` evidence relation; lift the owner-only tab rule to Owner/Admin |
| **Live Ledger** | LL0 to LL4 done, LL5 live verify pending, uncommitted | A **Ledger** tab in the live meeting side panel next to People and Chat; Promise Check-in raises the project's open and overdue promises and tasks; Agreement Check writes "agreed by N of M" decisions into the project log | Scope check-in to the project; otherwise unchanged |
| **Tasdeeq Verified Minutes** | T1 to T5 done in code, live study pending, uncommitted | An optional **Verify minutes** step between AI summary and task confirmation; attesters are project members who attended; the public seal check stays | Respect the 409 locks; reuse speaker claims for assignee evidence |

This framing answers the examiner's first question ("isn't this just Jira with Zoom?") with something no reviewed product shows: a promise spoken in Urdu-English on Monday, signed off by the people who said it, turned into a reviewed task, closed by a merged PR, and visible as kept from the original meeting.

## An eight-step build that extends the codebase in order

The plan follows the repo's existing convention (architect step first, one feature per step, prompt file and approval per step) under a new phase letter, W. ## What to cut so one loop works instead of nine half-tabs

The cut list removes breadth that adds risk without adding differentiation. Live captions leave the core (cost on Stream, no Urdu, Groq quota collision). The "development simulator" goes, or becomes a clearly labelled demo mode. The Contributors tab, the dashboard's personal GitHub contributions widget and per-member activity filters go, because they read as surveillance and add no proof. The separate global AI Assistant outside projects goes, since it doubles the cross-tenant retrieval risk; the existing personal AI chat can remain for meetings that belong to no project. Raw commit ingestion beyond PR-linked commits, sprints and milestones, appearance settings, and any digest beyond one daily email are out and should be stated as out. Existing screens without a PDF home fold in rather than vanish: Recordings into meeting detail, Prep into the project overview's "next meeting" card, Search into Ctrl K, and the Engagement tab stays per meeting and owner-visible, never aggregated per developer. Clerk Organizations, a second email or AI provider, and any paid Stream add-on stay excluded.

## Conclusion

The PDF's instinct is right but its emphasis is wrong: it spends 21 screens rebuilding what Huly, Linear and Atlassian already sell, and it leaves out the evidence trail that would make the product worth building. The research shifts the question from "can a student team build a development workspace?" to "can it prove one promise from spoken quote to merged code, safely?", and the codebase answers most of that already, since the ledger's quote validator, per-track bilingual transcripts and attestation are exactly the parts competitors lack. The practical implication is ordering: the security plumbing (membership helper, signed webhooks, project-filtered vector search) is cheap and must land before anything visible, because a single cross-project leak or forged "merged" event would undo the credibility the Proof Chain is meant to earn.

The second implication is that the uniqueness claim is perishable and should be worded and demonstrated accordingly: "none of the products we reviewed" carries a quote through review and a default-branch merge back to a kept promise, shown live with a real Urdu-English meeting and a planted partial PR that NeuraMeet correctly refuses to count as done. A narrow loop that survives an examiner trying to break it is worth more than nine tabs that each resemble something better funded.
