# Let Every Attendee Sign the Minutes

NeuraMeet's third signature feature should be **Tasdeeq: Verified Minutes**, a step after each meeting in which every attendee confirms, corrects, or formally records a dissent against the AI minutes attributed to them, after which the confirmed minutes are sealed so that any later change can be detected. The pitch for the panel: *"NeuraMeet's AI drafts the minutes, but the people who were in the room sign them off. Every line points to the transcript, every attendee approves what is said about them or records their own view, and the final minutes carry a fingerprint that shows if anyone edits them afterwards."* It wins on four counts. First, every product reviewed puts the **host** in charge of reviewing AI notes (Zoom lets the host edit, Webex emails the host, Fellow's redaction is run by the organiser), and none was found that gives each attendee a sign-off on their own attributed statements. Second, the failure it fixes is well documented: LLM meeting summaries still hallucinate, omit, and misattribute, under-represent minority positions, and users rarely check them unprompted. Third, it turns a real Pakistani rule into software. Federal Secretariat Instructions require decisions-only minutes, record individual views "only on request," and require minutes to be circulated and confirmed by members within fixed deadlines. Fourth, it costs **zero extra LLM calls per meeting** in its core form, because it folds into the existing `/api/ai/process` call and reuses the Promise Ledger's quote validator, the Live Ledger's voter identity, Resend email, `pdf-lib`, and Node's built-in `crypto`. It is clearly distinct from both ledgers: they track promises *across* meetings and the owner reviews them, while Tasdeeq makes the record *of one meeting* trustworthy and puts the review in the hands of every participant. The honest limits: re-processing currently deletes and rewrites insights, so it must be frozen first; NeuraMeet has no stored link between a speaker label and a user; and "tamper-evident" must never be stretched into "legally binding."

## Host-only review leaves the record unsigned

The 2025 to 2026 market has absorbed most of the meeting features a student would think of, so the third feature has to be chosen by elimination. Teams Facilitator runs the agenda with an on-stage timer, captures decisions and action items, and "summarizes key decisions and open questions" ([aguidetocloud](https://www.aguidetocloud.com/blog/microsoft-365-facilitator-agent/)). A planned August 2026 Facilitator update detects unanswered factual questions and answers them by web search ([windowsforum](https://windowsforum.com/news/microsoft-teams-facilitator-aug-2026-answers-unanswered-questions-with-web-search.430248/)); that rollout was not independently verified. Zoom AI Companion 3.0 does agentic search across past meetings and connectors and turns conversations into documents ([Zoom news](https://news.zoom.com/zoom-launches-ai-companion-3-0/)). Read.ai personalises recaps, surfaces last session's open items before recurring meetings ([read.ai](https://www.read.ai/meeting-reports)), scores turn-taking balance ([Read support](https://support.read.ai/hc/en-us/articles/4406653674003)), and recommends removing invitees or cancelling a series ([Read support](https://support.read.ai/hc/en-us/articles/7989314755731-What-kinds-of-meeting-recommendations-does-Read-make)). Fellow ships meeting-health analytics ([Fellow help](https://help.fellow.ai/en/articles/8940594-meeting-guideline-analytics)), and meeting-cost calculators with "move to async" advice are a commodity ([Flowtrace](https://www.flowtrace.co/collaboration-blog/best-meeting-cost-tools)). That rules out meeting cost and ROI, personalised recaps, airtime coaching, and open-question carry-forward as a headline.

What remains thin is **participant-side control of the record**. Every review flow found is host-centric. Zoom's host edits and shares the AI summary ([tldv guide](https://tldv.io/blog/meeting-notes-ultimate-guide/)). Webex emails the *host* to "review and share" the summary, action items, and transcript ([Webex help](https://help.webex.com/article/nslyqzx/Webex-App-%7C-AI-generated-meeting-summaries-in-recordings)). Fellow lets the organiser permanently redact lines "when meeting participants request that certain statements be removed," which shows the demand exists but routes it through one person ([Fellow help](https://help.fellow.ai/en/articles/10359653-redaction)). On integrity, the closest product is TrueScreen, a paid forensic bot that certifies meeting capture with qualified timestamps ([truescreen.io](https://truescreen.io/), vendor claim). No mainstream notetaker was found that exposes a "verify these minutes have not been altered" check. Legal commentary keeps pointing at the gap: a summary carries the authority of a record even though nobody signed it ([Smith Law](https://www.smithlaw.com/newsroom/publications/the-silent-guest-in-your-meetings-legal-risks-of-ai-note-takers)).

| Tasdeeq component | Closest existing product | Claim status |
|---|---|---|
| AI minutes drafted from the transcript | Every major tool | **Commodity.** Not claimed |
| Host edits or reviews AI minutes before sharing | Zoom, Webex, Fellow | **Commodity.** Not claimed |
| Every minutes line linked to its supporting transcript lines, unsupported lines flagged | FRAME and critique-and-refine research (model-side only) | **Unverified absence** in products |
| Each attendee confirms, corrects, or records a dissent on lines attributed to them | Not found; Fellow redaction is organiser-run | **Unverified absence.** Core novelty claim |
| "Record my view": a dissent entered on request, under the member's own name | Not found as a product; mirrors Secretariat Instructions | **Unverified absence** |
| Confirmed minutes sealed with a hash chain and a public verify page | TrueScreen (forensic, paid, bot-based) | **Absent among mainstream notetakers reviewed** |

Two gaps in the competitor check matter. Otter, Fireflies, tl;dv, Fathom, Avoma, and Granola changelogs were not read one by one, so absence rests on comparative searches; some allow comments on shared notes, which would narrow the claim only if those comments were tied to per-speaker attribution. Board-portal software (Diligent, BoardEffect) likely has manual minutes-approval workflows and was not researched, so the novelty claim must be scoped to **AI notetakers and meeting apps**.

## AI minutes fail quietly, and Pakistani bodies already require sign-off

The technical case is strong and recent. Findings of EMNLP 2025 reports that LLM meeting summaries "remain error-prone, often producing outputs with hallucinations, omissions, and irrelevancies," and its FRAME method keeps speaker attribution more consistent ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1094.pdf)). COLING 2025's "What's Wrong?" mimics human review with an identify-then-refine pass whose error taxonomy names **misattributed statements and omitted necessary mentions** ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.143/)), and the same work notes that summarisers inherit upstream ASR and attribution errors. A COLING 2025 industry paper shows ROUGE and BERTScore are weak proxies, leaving human evaluation as the gold standard ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-industry.48.pdf)). Across 18 LLMs, DeliberationBank (3,000 participants, 4,500 annotators) found **persistent underrepresentation of minority positions** in deliberation summaries ([arXiv 2510.05154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05154)), the strongest evidence that dissent in particular gets dropped. Upstream, Whisper hallucinated on 1.4% of audio segments in 2023 experiments, and 38% of those hallucinations contained explicit harms such as false associations ([Careless Whisper, FAccT 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.08021)). On code-switched Urdu-English, reported WER rises to **0.532 against 0.289** without switching ([arXiv 2508.09865](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09865)), a figure seen only in snippets. Softer numbers exist (a survey finding 30.4% of AI summaries contained a factual error ([note.com](https://note.com/ai_scout_jp/n/n99f638fec546?hl=en))), but their methodology is unverified and they should stay out of the thesis.

Model-side fixes leave one problem unsolved: someone still has to check. A CHI 2025 survey of 319 knowledge workers found that **the more people trust the AI, the less critical thinking they apply**, often because checking felt too trivial ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf)). Verification therefore has to be built into the workflow and assigned to the person with the most at stake, which is the speaker whose words are being summarised. Ownership research adds a design rule: people disown work when they only approve AI suggestions and keep ownership when they rewrite it ([arXiv 2609.20658](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20658v1)). So Tasdeeq lets attendees *correct* and *add*, not merely click approve.

The Pakistani case is what makes this an FYP rather than a generic feature. The federal **Secretariat Instructions**, issued under the Rules of Business 1973, require "brief minutes recorded mentioning only the salient points considered and the decisions taken," and state that "individual view points should not be recorded except on request" ([Establishment Division](https://www.establishment.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/Secretariat%20Instructions%20Edition%202021.pdf)); Punjab has its own 2023 manual ([Punjab PDF](https://schools.punjab.gov.pk/system/files/MANUAL%20OF%20SECRETARIAT%20INSTRUCTIONS%20(Updated%202023)_1_0.pdf)). Privatisation Commission regulations circulate approved minutes to members **"for confirmation within three working days"** ([privatisation.gov.pk](https://privatisation.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/Regulations_Conduct_Board_Meeting2019_09042021).pdf)), and the Council of Common Interests sends minutes within **seven working days** ([CCI Rules](https://ipc.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/CCI/Rules%20of%20Procedure%20of%20the%20CCI,%202010%20-%20amended%2022_4_2021.pdf)). University statutory bodies likely follow the same pattern, though no HEC document was retrieved to confirm it. Tasdeeq is a direct digital version of that procedure: decisions-only minutes, a confirmation deadline, and a "Record my view" button that implements "on request" literally.

The power-distance evidence explains why the dissent mechanism is user-initiated and attributed. Among Sargodha school teachers, **power distance positively predicted employee silence** and moderated the effect of abusive leadership ([IJELM](https://hipatiapress.com/hpjournals/index.php/ijelm/article/view/10373)), and the silence-leadership link was stronger in Pakistan than in Australia ([University of Wollongong thesis](https://ro.uow.edu.au/articles/thesis/Influence_of_followers_voice_and_silence_on_leaders_ethical_behaviour/30749036)). The CHI 2026 meeting-inclusion field study found that organisations pushed an inclusion tool toward **private reflection rather than feedback exchange** ([Houtti et al., arXiv 2601.11750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11750)). A procedural right to record one's view, after the meeting and away from the live room, fits that culture better than a public nudge or an anonymous AI dissenter. It is also consistent with the earlier finding that anonymous AI-mediated dissent lowered junior members' psychological safety, which is why the devil's advocate was rejected.

## How Tasdeeq differs from both ledgers and the runners-up

The risk with any third feature is that the panel reads it as "the ledger again." The distinction is in object, actor, and time. The **Promise Ledger** tracks *promises and decisions across meetings*: it asks whether something said in meeting 1 was kept in meeting 3, and only the **owner** confirms or rejects each match. **Live Ledger** brings those promises *into the call* and runs a one-tap Agreement Check on a single decision. **Tasdeeq** concerns the *whole record of one meeting*: summary, key points, decisions, and action items, reviewed by **every attendee** for the lines that concern them, *after* the call, and then frozen with a verifiable seal. It answers a different question: not "did we keep our word?" but "is this an accurate, agreed record of what was said?"

| | Promise Ledger | Live Ledger | Tasdeeq |
|---|---|---|---|
| Object | Promises and decisions | Past promises; one new decision | The full minutes of one meeting |
| Time | After, across meetings | During the call | After the call, before the record is final |
| Who acts | Owner confirms or rejects matches | Owner raises cards; all vote on one decision | Every attendee confirms, corrects, or records a view |
| Output | Kept / open / overdue / reversed with quote | Live marks and "agreed by 3 of 4" | Evidence-linked minutes, per-member confirmation status, sealed hash |
| AI role | Extract and match | Rank and rewrite | Draft and link evidence; humans finalise |

The two features reinforce each other without overlapping. A Live Ledger "Not yet" vote is a ready-made, non-LLM dissent signal that Tasdeeq can show in the minutes under the voter's name. Ledger items are part of the sealed bundle, so a promise's evidence cannot be silently edited later. Tasdeeq also stays clear of the earlier runners-up. It is not *While You Were Gone*, which catches up a person who dropped out. It is not *Viva/FYP Panel Mode*, which is an education-specific record. It is not *Urdu-English meeting intelligence*, which is about language quality; the uncertainty-aware "code-switch confidence lens" belongs to that runner-up and is deliberately left out of Tasdeeq's core. And its dissent mechanism is the opposite of the rejected *anonymous devil's advocate*: human, attributed, and initiated by the member.

## A six-step build that freezes, links, attests, then seals

The feature fits the codebase with one hard prerequisite and one identity gap. The prerequisite is that `POST /api/ai/process` **deletes and re-inserts** `meeting_insights`, `action_items`, and `follow_ups` on every run (`src/app/api/ai/process/route.ts:63-215`), so a re-process would silently wipe confirmed minutes. Re-processing must either be blocked once review starts or produce a new draft version. The identity gap is that nothing stores which Clerk user a speaker label belongs to: generated transcripts resolve labels from `attendance_events.participant_name` in memory and discard the link, and since the F4 wiring was removed they reach AI only as re-uploaded `.txt` files with arbitrary labels. The cheapest robust fix needs no model at all. On first opening the review page, each attendee **claims their speaker label** from the list ("Which of these is you?"), and the owner can assign the rest. Access is already settled: `GET /api/meetings/[id]` lets the owner or any participant recorded in `attendance_events` read a meeting.

| Step | What it does | Reuses | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 `/architect tasdeeq` | Lock schema (`minutes_versions`, `minutes_attestations` keyed by meeting, version, and attendee; `minutes_seals` with `content_sha256`, `prev_seal_sha256`, `signature`, `key_id`), lock routes, and add them to the AGENTS.md API Contracts list before coding. Decide the freeze rule and the quorum rule ("owner plus N of M, or deadline passes") | S0 and LL0 spec pattern | S |
| T1 Evidence-linked minutes | Extend the single `/api/ai/process` structured call so each summary point, key point, and decision returns supporting line numbers, and add a `dissent[]` field (topic, position, quote, speaker, line). Validate every quote against `raw_text` with the Ledger's validator; flag lines with no support as "unsupported" | `generateStructuredWithFallback`, `writeLedgerItems` quote check, numbered-line format in `ledger.ts` | S |
| T2 Freeze and versions | Snapshot insights into `minutes_versions` as version 1; make re-process create a new draft instead of deleting | `meeting_insights`, `action_items` | M |
| T3 Attest | A Minutes tab: claim your speaker label, then for each line attributed to you (or naming you as assignee) choose Confirm, Correct (with a note), or Record my view. Show a per-member status and a deadline countdown. Resend sends "minutes ready for your review" | `resolveLiveCaller` and `verifyStreamUserToken` identity pattern, `attendance_events`, `sendMeetingReadyEmail`, `useLoadingTimeout` and `LoadError` | M |
| T4 Seal and verify | When the quorum or deadline is met, the owner seals: hash a canonical JSON of the minutes, transcript SHA-256, ledger items, and attestations; chain it to the owner's previous seal; sign with Ed25519. Email the hash to every attendee (the external anchor), embed it and a verify URL in a PDF, and serve a read-only verify page | Node `crypto`, `pdf-lib` (attendance report pattern), Resend | S to M |
| T5 Evaluate | Seeded-error study and demo data (below) | Promise Ledger demo meetings | S |

Corrections are stored as attributed annotations next to the AI text rather than as silent rewrites, which keeps both the AI draft and the human correction visible and makes the evaluation possible. One optional LLM call can re-check a correction against the transcript and attach the supporting quote, in the Ledger's exact-quote style.

**Cost per meeting.** The core adds **zero LLM calls**: evidence links and dissent ride in the existing structured call as extra output tokens. Embeddings: zero. The optional correction re-check adds one small call per correction. Email: about two Resend messages per attendee (review request, sealed hash), so a five-person meeting uses about 10 of the 3,000 free monthly emails, roughly 300 meetings a month. Storage is a few kilobytes of JSON and hashes per meeting, trivial against Supabase Free's **500 MB database** ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Hashing and signing use Node's built-in `crypto`, so no extension, no blockchain, and no paid notarisation are needed. Every call stays inside a single Vercel function well under the **300-second Hobby limit** ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations)).

The real constraint is the shared LLM budget, not this feature. On Groq's free plan, the fallback `openai/gpt-oss-20b` allows **8K tokens per minute and 200K per day** ([Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)), and a 16,000-character transcript already uses roughly 4K to 5K tokens, so extra output fields push the fallback path closer to one meeting per minute. Gemini's free quota is unclear: Google no longer publishes free-tier numbers ([Gemini API](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits)), and aggregators disagree between **20 requests per day** for current Flash models ([ScriptByAI](https://www.scriptbyai.com/gemini-api-free-tier-limits/)) and about 1,500. The project's AI Studio page must be checked before the demo, which is exactly why folding into the existing call matters.

## A demo the panel can break, and the risks it will raise

The demo should let a panellist try to tamper with the record. Use a seeded meeting from the Promise Ledger demo with two browsers, the owner and a member.

| Time | Action | What the panel sees |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | "The Ledger remembers promises, Live Ledger brings them into the call. Tasdeeq makes sure the record itself is one everyone agreed to." | The Minutes tab in Draft, one line flagged "unsupported by transcript" |
| 0:30–1:30 | Member claims "Ayesha" as their speaker label | Only Ayesha's three attributed lines are highlighted, each with its transcript quote |
| 1:30–2:30 | Ayesha corrects one line and taps **Record my view** on a decision: "Vercel free tier theek hai, lekin backup plan chahiye" | The correction appears beside the AI text; the decision shows "Recorded view: Ayesha" under her name |
| 2:30–3:15 | Owner confirms; quorum met; owner seals | Status "Confirmed by 2 of 2"; hash shown and arrives in both inboxes; PDF downloads with hash and verify link |
| 3:15–4:00 | A panellist edits one word in Supabase directly, then opens the verify page | **"Altered after sealing"**, with the hash mismatch shown against the emailed hash |

The evaluation should combine an objective metric with a small study, as the earlier features do. The objective layer is a **seeded-error catch rate**: plant k known errors (a misattributed line, a changed date, a dropped objection) in the draft minutes and measure how many participants catch with Tasdeeq versus a host-only review. Alongside it, report the share of minutes lines that are evidence-supported before and after T1, the dissent-extraction precision on English and code-mixed transcripts against two labellers with Cohen's kappa, and time to full confirmation. The study layer is 6 to 10 participants in mock FYP-group meetings, comparing host-only review against Tasdeeq with SUS and a trust rating. Claim task-level gains only.

The panel will raise these risks.

**Nobody approves.** Confirmation can stall. Use a deadline (three or seven working days, mirroring the government rules) and a quorum such as "owner plus N of M." Label anything sealed after a deadline as "confirmed by 2 of 4; 2 did not respond" rather than "deemed confirmed."

**The operator can re-sign.** Server-side signing proves only that NeuraMeet's key signed the record; the student running the server could re-seal altered minutes. The defence is the external anchor: the hash goes to every attendee by email at seal time and is printed in the PDF, so a re-sealed record no longer matches what attendees hold. Never call it legally binding, notarised, or blockchain-grade; "tamper-evident" is the accurate term.

**The model sees only the first 16,000 characters.** `MAX_TRANSCRIPT_CHARS` means the tail of a meeting longer than about 30 to 40 minutes is never summarised, and dissent there is lost. Either add a chunked pass (more calls against the Groq budget) or show a coverage banner ("minutes cover lines 1 to 312 of 480") so the record is honest about what it covers. The banner fits the feature's spirit and costs nothing.

**Quotes from recorded Urdu meetings are translations.** The generated-transcript cleanup step requires the "final output MUST be entirely in English" (`transcript-generation.ts:64`). Until an untranslated copy is stored alongside, an attendee confirming their "exact quote" is confirming a translation. Scope Urdu claims to uploaded transcripts or store `original_text` for recorded ones.

**Guests and uploaded meetings.** Guests have no Clerk account, and Live Ledger's guest-token identity is designed around the live call; whether it works after the call ends is unverified, so guest attestation may need an emailed one-time link. Upload-only meetings have no attendance records, so the owner alone signs, which should be shown, not hidden.

**Attributing dissent is sensitive.** An LLM may invent "dissent" in a consensus meeting or tilt toward majority views. Quote validation against `raw_text` blocks fabrication, AI-extracted dissent is labelled as a suggestion until the quoted person confirms it, and only "Record my view" entries count as formal dissent.

**It looks like the ledger's confirm/reject.** It reuses the review pattern on purpose. The answer is the comparison table above: different object, different actor, different moment, plus an integrity layer the ledgers do not have.

The defensible claim, in the narrow wording AGENTS.md already uses: *"None of the major meeting apps or AI notetakers we reviewed let each attendee confirm, correct, or formally record their own view against the AI minutes attributed to them, and then seal the confirmed minutes so that any later change is detectable."* Say "in our review" every time, and cite TrueScreen and board-portal software as related work.

## Three runners-up, ranked

**Runner-up 1: Offline-Safe Voice.** The browser records the user's own microphone locally in small chunks during a call; if the connection drops, recording continues, and on reconnect the chunks upload to Supabase Storage, are transcribed with Groq Whisper, and merge into the transcript as recovered speech. It is distinct from While You Were Gone, which helps someone catch up on what *others* said; this protects what *you* said. The local evidence is strong: an August 2025 PTCL backbone outage cut national internet to about 20% of normal ([Digital Rights Monitor](https://digitalrightsmonitor.pk/pakistan-internet-outage-2025/)), load shedding drains tower batteries and drops mobile signal ([PhoneWorld](https://www.phoneworld.com.pk/load-shedding-disrupts-mobile-and-internet-services-across-pakistan/)), and users are mobile-first with **68% smartphone adoption** ([CW Pakistan](https://cwpakistan.com/pta-annual-report-2024-25-shows-growth-in-telecom-subscribers-revenues-and-infrastructure/)). The metric, share of speech recovered under throttled-network trials, is clean. It ranks second because its riskiest layer is the one that has hurt this project most: browser `MediaRecorder` behaviour alongside Stream's media handling is unverified, and the Groq Whisper budget of **28,800 audio-seconds per day** ([Groq](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)) plus 1 GB of free storage cap how many recovered minutes the demo can afford.

**Runner-up 2: Meeting Purpose and Goal Attainment.** This would productise Microsoft Research's intentionality line: a pre-meeting purpose prompt via `/api/prep`, a passive in-call goal indicator, and a post-meeting "goal met, partly, or not" verdict backed by transcript evidence. The research base is real: the Meeting Purpose Assistant improved preparation through brief reflective dialogue ([arXiv 2505.14370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14370)), and passive cues helped people stay on track without intruding ([arXiv 2504.01082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01082)). It ranks third because a preregistered field experiment across 7,196 meetings found goal nudges did **not** significantly improve meeting effectiveness ([arXiv 2602.16939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16939)), Teams Facilitator already covers agendas and timekeeping, and it has no Pakistan-specific hook.

**Runner-up 3: Cross-meeting Fact Drift.** This would detect when figures, dates, or requirements are stated differently across meetings, a need voiced directly as "contradiction detection" on Microsoft Q&A ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/2336737/ai-meeting-agent-for-transcript-summarization-cont)), with only DIY tutorials as solutions ([DEV](https://dev.to/alichherawalla/how-to-find-contradictions-between-meeting-notes-and-a-project-brief-in-2026-571d)). It is cheap as a new ledger `kind`, but that is its weakness: it reads as a Promise Ledger variant next to the existing "reversed" status, and checking against documents needs a second RAG corpus.

Dropped after checking: meeting cost and async suggestions (Read.ai, Fellow, Flowtrace), personalised recaps (Read.ai, and blocked here by the missing speaker map), airtime and inclusion coaching (Read.ai, and overlapping NeuraMeet's own engagement insights), unanswered-question tracking (Teams Facilitator, Read.ai), a successor handover brief (overlaps Prep and Search; no team model), and a WhatsApp-ready handoff, which is a good companion to Tasdeeq (share the sealed minutes with a link back to the official record) but too thin to be a signature.

## Conclusion

The three signature features now form one argument about trust in AI meeting records. The Promise Ledger says *the system remembers*, Live Ledger says *it brings that memory into the room*, and Tasdeeq says *the record is one the people in the room agreed to and nobody can quietly change*. Each depends on the one before: evidence-linked quotes come from the ledger machinery, attributed votes come from Live Ledger, and Tasdeeq seals them all. That dependency chain is the strongest uniqueness argument, because a competitor would need all three layers to copy the last one.

The less obvious lesson is that the most defensible AI feature in this set is the one where the AI gives up the final word. The research says model-side fixes do not remove the need for human checking and that people will not check unprompted; Pakistani procedure already assigns that check to members with deadlines. Building the AI around that procedure, rather than replacing it, gives the panel something rare in an FYP: a measurable claim (errors caught), a local rule implemented literally, and a demo where an examiner can try to break the record and watch it refuse.
